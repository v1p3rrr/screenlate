# Group 5e: Cantonese, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Marathi (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage F, rows "Group 5", "Group 5 research",
"Group 5 release"). That plan's decision rows bind here; the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name.
This file holds the block's research, decisions, questions and progress (question numbers are its own);
`notes/languages.md` points here. Nothing is built before the owner says so; the block is released provisionally
after the big release.

## Goal

- Cantonese: a language of its own with the Chinese text rules (Han characters, no spaces, the Chinese lookup and
  script conversion), Jyutping readings, Cantonese dictionaries (words.hk, CC-Canto, CC-CEDICT with Cantonese
  readings), Hong Kong glyph forms, Cantonese recordings and voice, translation as `yue`.
- Tamil, Telugu, Kannada: Brahmic scripts new to the app, spaced, handled as Hindi: Wiktionary dictionaries,
  Tesseract on the phone and the Paddle recognizers once the waiting models are released, recordings and Google's
  voices.
- Marathi: Devanagari as Hindi; ML Kit's Devanagari model, bundled for Hindi, reads it.

### Questions from the build (2026-10-29)

Written while the owner is away; each is decided provisionally as built and stays open until the owner answers.

18. **Syllable initials: one written syllable. Open, decided provisionally (2026-10-29): (a), as built.** Question 8
    said a one- or two-syllable word before a period does not end the Anki sentence. In Marathi a written syllable is
    an akshara: a consonant or conjunct with its vowel sign (पु, श्री, प्रा) or a vowel. 64% of Marathi news sentences end
    with a two-akshara word before the period (आहे 29,976 of 95,254, होते, नाही, केली), so the rule with two syllables
    would join almost every sentence to the next. (a, recommended) one akshara: पु. ल. देशपांडे, प.पू., ह.भ.प. keep the
    sentence going, two-syllable abbreviations only by the list (सायं, उदा); dialogue words that end sentences are
    हो, तो, मी, तू, ती and the short imperatives जा, ये, घे, दे, ने, या, घ्या, द्या, न्या, व्हा (98 घ्या and 63 द्या at the
    end of news sentences); (b) two aksharas, as the words of question 8 say. Changing it is one number.
19. **Marathi table headers: by name, for Marathi only. Open, decided provisionally (2026-10-29): (a), as built.**
    mr-en has 8 form headwords that are labels of Wiktionary's verb tables (होकारार्थी "affirmative", नकारार्थी
    "negative", Progressive, "m f n" and others), each pointing to all 238 tabled verbs (1,904 rows): a tap on
    होकारार्थी would list 714 forms. The plan said "form headwords pointing to more than 50 lemmas"; counted over every
    wty dictionary in the archive, 36 others have such headwords, many of them right (Thai classifiers คน, ตัว;
    German articles in de-fr's tables; Dutch pronouns in nl-en's). (a, recommended) the 8 labels dropped by name for
    Marathi (`HeadwordMarks.droppedForms`); (b) every form headword naming more than 50 lemmas dropped in every
    language, after a check of what each of the 36 loses. Changing to (b): a counting pass at import.
20. **Marathi verb endings: the imperfective participle only. Open, decided provisionally (2026-10-29): (a), as
    built.** Question 5 chose noun endings. On the emulator करत ("doing", 42nd commonest news word) found nothing:
    Wiktionary gives forms for 238 verbs only, and none of them lists the participle. Among the 20,000 commonest news
    words without an entry, verb stem + ending → the infinitive in -णे finds: -त 122 words (करत, होत, जात, येत; 14,334
    tokens), -ताना 39 (करताना, असताना), -तात 27, -तो 23, -ते 20, -ता 40, -ला 60, -ली 65, -ले 73, -ून 98 (दिसून,
    पाहून). The participles meet no word with an entry and nothing wrong in the list; the others find some wrong words

## Long lines and cost

- A 16-unit window of these scripts gives 6-9 candidates (Latin text 2-4), almost all dropped after the lookup
  because they end before a mark (Hindi has the same); text processors add at most 2^k variants for k processors that
  change the text (ta 3, kn 3, te 2, mr up to 6), usually 1-2. Import keys cost nothing at lookup. The endings cost a
  lookup per matching ending per candidate, like Bengali's.
