# Group 5c: Finnish, Estonian, Hungarian, Azerbaijani, Uzbek (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage F). That plan's decision rows bind here;
the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there. This file holds the
block's research, decisions, questions and progress (row "Group 5 research"); question numbers are its own.

## Goal

Five languages that stack endings on a stem, written in Latin letters (Uzbek also in Cyrillic), each looked up, read
by OCR, voiced and exported as the group 3 and 4a languages are:

- Finnish (fi): the largest Wiktionary form tables of any language (4.5 million rows in fi-en), our own list of
  possessive suffixes and clitics, compound parts as German.
- Hungarian (hu): full form tables in hu-en, our own rule for verb prefixes, compound parts; ő and ű, which OCR reads
  as ö and ü, matched at lookup.
- Estonian (et): form tables in et-en, a whole-word rule for the negative forms of olema, compound parts.
- Azerbaijani (az): form tables in az-en, our own Turkic ending rules, Turkish casing of i and İ.
- Uzbek (uz), Latin: small dictionaries, our own Turkic ending rules, ʻ and ʼ normalized and matched when OCR drops
  them; Cyrillic text read through a transliteration to Latin.

For all five: the English dictionary's form rows reach the user's other dictionaries; without English it is
installed as a "Word forms" item (question 4).

### Yomitan at e7334a9

| lang | descriptor | text processors | transforms |
|---|---|---|---|
| fi | `fin`, example lukea | capitalization | none |
| et | `est`, example lugema | capitalization | none |
| hu | `hun`, example olvasni | capitalization | none |
| az | none | – | – |
| uz | none | – | – |

- az is borrowed from Turkish's descriptor under its own code (`aze`), as Malay's is from Indonesian's; it needs
  Turkish casing (İ ↔ i, I ↔ ı), so glue.js's `replacedProcessors.tr` applies to az too. uz can be borrowed from
  Turkish as well (plain capitalization is enough; Turkish casing would be wrong: Uzbek writes I/i as in English), or
  from Finnish; the borrowing check needs a descriptor without transforms, which all of these are.
- Word resolution never shortens a single word: `_getNextSubstring` drops whole trailing words, so without rules a
  word is found only as written or through a form-of row. Checked in Node with the Turkish bundle: "kitaplarımızdan"
  gives only itself and its capitalized form. The table row of `notes/languages.md` that gives Malay "form-of rows and
  the longest start" (line 556) is wrong in the same way as the line group 4e corrected.
- Rules of our own (`own-transforms.js`, `addedTransforms`) carry conditions; Wiktionary's rows give no rules, so a
  deinflected text matches an entry without a part of speech only through glue.js's `untypedRules` (Bengali's).
  Stacked endings chain through conditions that encode the slot order (group 4e's rules), not by stripping anything
  in any order.

### Lookup coverage

Method and the full table: the shared note of the group sessions, summarized here. Gold lemmas from the UD test sets
(proper nouns apart; compound marks removed; az and uz verb stems mapped to the infinitive), running text from
Leipzig's news 2020 10K samples (lowercase words). Steps, each only where the ones before found nothing: exact
headword (with case variants; az Turkish casing; uz apostrophes normalized); form-of rows (a second step from a lemma
without definitions); our own ending lists; the longest matching start, measured but not built.

- News words found by form rows (with own endings): fi-en 91.8 (93.6); fi-ru alone 37.0, with fi-en's forms 80.0
  (82.6); et-en 75.1, et-ru alone 23.1, with et-en's forms 49.2; hu-en 85.5 (87.0), hu-ru alone 27.2, with forms
  47.7; az-en 83.3 (91.6), az-ru alone 32.5 (62.6), with az-en's forms 57.9 (66.9); uz-en 48.8 (64.2), uz-ru 33.2
  (49.6), with uz-en's forms 38.4 (50.6).
- Own-ending hits checked by hand: fi about 97% right, az about 90%, uz about 70% (wrong: ham → ha, oldida → olmoq,
  ichida → ichmoq); a minimum stem of three letters removes the worst. Group 4e found that slot-ordered rules (noun:
  stem, plural, possessive, case, predicative; verb chains checked against entries tagged `v`) raise precision over
  stripping any ending in any order (Kazakh 36/40 against 32/40, Kyrgyz 37/40 against 17/40).
- A rule set learned from the dictionary's own form rows adds 1-3 points at most: what is left is derivation,
  clitics, possessives and words the dictionaries lack.
- The longest matching start gives the right lemma for 1-12% of the words it reaches; the rest are roots of words
  the dictionary lacks or junk (hoopis → hoo, uchun → uch). Not built (question 2).
- Finnish: possessive suffixes and clitics are missing from the tables (talossani "in my house", rahatkin "the money
  too"): clitics -kaan/-kään, -kin, -han/-hän, -ko/-kö, -pa/-pä stripped first, then possessives -ni, -si, -nsa/-nsä,
  -mme, -nne and the long-vowel -Vn (talossaan); the stem is tried as is and with -n (the genitive and the
  nominative plural lose it before a possessive), and -kse with -ksi (translative).
- Hungarian: a verb with a prefix (meg-, el-, ki-, be-, fel-, le-, át-, rá-, össze-, vissza- …, 25 in all) that has no
  table of its own is found through the bare verb's forms; the result is the bare verb.
- Estonian: the endings are in the tables; the negative forms pole, polnud, poleks, poldud lead to olema (0.3% of
  words); -gi/-ki words are almost all headwords of their own (ikkagi, veelgi).
- Azerbaijani, Uzbek: case, possessive, plural, predicative and verb endings; az in its two- and four-way vowel
  harmony, uz without harmony; consonant changes undone (az çörəyi → çörək, ayağı → ayaq; uz -gʻi → -q, kitobi as
  is); a verb stem gets the infinitive (az -maq/-mək, uz -moq).

### Compounds

`Compounds.kt` (fewest parts, then the longest shortest part, parts of at least 3 letters, at most 12 lookups) on
the words the steps above miss: it fires on 4-6.5% of Finnish news words, 6.6% Estonian, 5% Hungarian. On the UD sets
the boundaries were right for 48% of the Finnish splits and 57% of the Estonian ones. Wrong Finnish splits are mostly
possessive and clitic forms (tuttava+ani), which the ending list catches first; Estonian derived words (uuri+mus for
uurimus); Hungarian verb forms (megtart+ott). Linking elements: none to skip (Finnish genitive first parts,
kirjaston+hoitaja, are form rows; Estonian genitive stems likewise).

### Uzbek Cyrillic

- Uzbek is official in Latin; Cyrillic stays in older books, some sites and signs. In the news sample 84 of 148k
  tokens are Cyrillic; Leipzig's 2011 crawl is 68% Cyrillic.
- A transliteration of our own (е → ye at a word start and after a vowel, ъ or ь; ц → ts after a vowel, else s;
  ғ → gʻ, ў → oʻ, ъ → ʼ, ь dropped) gives the Latin spelling of 382 of the 387 Cyrillic spellings that uz-en and uz-ru
  list with their Latin headword; the other five are dictionary errors.
