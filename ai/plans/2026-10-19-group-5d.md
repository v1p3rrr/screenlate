# Group 5d: Bulgarian, Macedonian, Mongolian (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage F). That plan's decision rows bind here;
the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there. This file holds the
block's research, decisions, questions and progress (row "Group 5 research"); question numbers are its own.

## Goal

Three Cyrillic-script languages, each looked up, read by OCR, voiced and exported as the group 3 and 4a languages are:

- Bulgarian (bg): Yomitan's descriptor (capitalization), our own ending list for the postfixed article and other
  endings, PaddleOCR's Cyrillic model on the phone.
- Macedonian (mk): no Yomitan descriptor; built from Bulgarian's under its own code. Wiktionary's form tables cover
  it; letters PaddleOCR misreads are fixed after recognition or tried again at lookup.
- Mongolian (mn), Cyrillic only: Yomitan's descriptor (capitalization), our own ending list with vowel-harmony
  variants and stem rules, PaddleOCR's Cyrillic model.

  1,729 (inside `needsCount`'s band), mn→zh 1,923. mk-en is 8,260,066 bytes, just under `COUNTED_BELOW`.
- No own-language edition (bg-bg, mk-mk, mn-mn) and no glossaries. Transcriptions (`dict/all/<lang>/wty-<lang>-ipa.zip`)
  pass for all three: bg 0.60 MB 50.7k words, mk 0.86 MB 69.4k, mn 0.10 MB 5.3k; no stress marks in them.
- Headwords are NFC and have no readings. Form rows: bg-en 292,865 of 314,908 rows, mk-en 736,695 of 781,521, mn-en
  20,346 of 25,236; the →ru pairs have few (bg-ru 13,884, mk-ru 87, mn-ru 22).
- Stress marks: 243,815 bg-en headwords carry U+0301 (кни́гата, сто́лът, но́вият), 188,773 of them with no unmarked
  twin, and form rows name stressed lemmas (книгата → кни́га, itself a form row → книга): stripping the acute from form
  headwords and lemma names at import (`HeadwordMarks`, as Russian) makes them findable; without it the bg-en
  headwords reach 740 of wordfreq's top 1,000 Bulgarian words, with it 923. mk-en 1,820 and mn-en 1,282 (Russian
  loans) carry U+0301 too. bg-ru writes stress with ѐ (53) and ѝ (48) as well (паркѐт), which an acute-only rule
  leaves. A rule that decomposed and dropped every U+0301 would turn ѓ ќ into г к in 16,387 mk-en headwords; the
  existing rule drops marks after vowels only, as with Serbo-Croatian ć.
- ѝ: bg-en has the pronoun ѝ as its own entry, и as its own, and й → ѝ <alternative common nonstandard>; mk-en has ѝ,
  сѐ, нѐ, "сѐ уште", "пред сѐ" as own entries. wordfreq: small_bg has й at rank 152 (the pronoun typed as й) and ѝ at
  551; in Macedonian сè with a Latin è (rank 246) is more frequent than сѐ (479).
- Bulgarian articles and verbs: all 122 forms of 37 nouns, 62 of 8 adjectives and 55 of 8 verbs are form rows (30, 38
  and 9 only after stress removal). Comparatives are rows of their own with the hyphen (по́-голя́м → голям
  <comparative>, по-добре → добре́); 24,215 bg-en headwords start with по-/най-.
- Macedonian: every probed form is a headword (градот, градов, градон; книгата, книгава, книгана; читам, читаат,
  читаше; поголем, најголем, written joined). 152k mk-en form items (13,339 lemmas) lead to a lemma that is itself
  only a form row (Јапончето → Јапонче → Јапонец <diminutive>); the engine's second form-of step (from a lemma
  without definitions, `candidate_lookup.cpp`) reaches them, and on the treebanks below it changes the measured
  shares by under 0.1 point. 3,205 items name their lemma with a gender left on ("куќа f"), which no step reaches.
  bg-en has 5,126 items (907 lemmas) of the first kind.
- Mongolian: 11 of the probed nouns have full case tables, most have none (ээж, нүд, нохой); 14 of 46 verb forms are
  rows. The traditional script: 2,425 headwords, 1,864 rows pointing to the Cyrillic word.
- Data faults: exact duplicate rows (mk-en 1,438), template residue ("{{{1}}}ът" in bg-ru, 98 rows in mn-en), bare
  endings as bg-ru headwords (и, а, ите), 12,248 bg-en items tagged "comparative" on positive forms (иновацио́нната
  → иновационен <… comparative …>, ѝ → неин <comparative short-form>), "subjective/objective" on plural definite
  forms, mn-en's -аад converb tagged "locative imperfect", 44 mk-en and 102 bg-en form headwords with a Latin
  look-alike inside (своjот, лединкsа).

- By the recommendation rule (row "OCR engine choice") PaddleOCR's Cyrillic model is recommended for all three: 6,
  25 and 0 character errors against Tesseract fast's 104, 115 and 174. Tesseract fast and best are as accurate as
  each other, so fast is the one marked "faster". The eslav model (Russian, Ukrainian) lacks ѝ and does worse on
  Bulgarian; it is not offered.
- Every engine reads Bulgarian ѝ as й (cloud, PaddleOCR) or и (Tesseract, PaddleOCR on Times); straight quotes for “.
- A sheet of every Macedonian letter in both cases (Arial, Times) shows PaddleOCR's errors: Ѕ ѕ always as Latin S s;
  Ќ as К (in Times also ќ as к: керка); Љ as Л or ЛЬ; Ѓ as Ґ, and in Times ѓ as Ғ and Ѓ as Ї; Ѐ ѐ as Е е, é, è or ё;
  Ѝ ѝ as Й й or и. Lowercase љ њ џ ѓ ќ in Arial and every Џ Њ Ј are read right. The cloud reads all of them right
  but Ѐ ѐ (È è, Latin) and Ѝ ѝ (Й й or и), and once a lone Ј as Latin J.
- Mongolian ө ү are read right by PaddleOCR and the cloud on every screen; no Latin letter appeared.
- On the 2026-10-16/17 screens PaddleOCR cyrillic made 2.7-4.3% errors on hard Russian, Ukrainian, Kazakh and Serbian
  screens; these three are in the same range or better.

### Text

- Bulgarian quotes „…“ (“ closes), also «…» and ‚…‘ inside; Macedonian „…“ and ‚…‘; Mongolian news write “…”
  (949 and 929 of them in 10,000 sentences) and straight quotes (334), «…» twice.
- Ordinals take a hyphen, not a period, in all three (Bulgarian 5-и, 21-ви, 3-та; Macedonian 5-ти, 2-ри; Mongolian
  2-р, 15-нд, 19-ийн), so row "Ordinals with a period" does not apply.
- Mongolian initials are written joined to the name (Х.Баттулга: 900 times against 4 with a space in the news
  sample); a period before a letter already keeps the sentence going. Abbreviations with periods are rare there (г.м.
  for "etc." usually ends a sentence).
- Abbreviations that keep a sentence going (lists to finish at implementation, as Russian's): Bulgarian г., гр., с.,
  ул., бул., пл., обл., проф., доц., акад., инж., т.е., напр., вж., стр., чл., ал., т., св., хил., млн., млрд., в.,
  пр.н.е. (letters н, е paired), сл.н.е., лв.; Macedonian г., в., ул., бул., с., проф., доц., акад., т.е., т.н.,
  нпр., стр., бр., чл., ст., ден., мил., илј., пр.н.е., н.е., св.; "и т.н.", "и др.", "итн." usually end a sentence
  and are left out. Bulgarian д-р and Macedonian м-р have no period.
- Word lengths: 0.19% of Bulgarian treebank words, none of the Macedonian ones and 0.05% of Mongolian news words are
  longer than 16 letters, so the scan length stays 16 (row "Scan length" of `2026-10-19-group-4b.md`).

### Lookup

- Spaced languages look words up whole (`SearchResolution.WORD`): only compound parts use letter resolution. The
  "longest matching start" fallback that `notes/languages.md` names for Turkish, Kazakh and Malay is not built (group
  5c found the same); forms are found through form-of rows or an ending list only.
- Coverage on UD treebanks (gold lemma per word; punctuation, numbers and names left out), exact lookup plus form-of
  rows (lemma names with stress marks removed; a second step from form-only lemmas changes nothing above 0.1
  point); "ceiling" is the share whose gold lemma is a headword:

  | treebank | dictionaries | words | found | right lemma | ceiling |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | UD Bulgarian BTB test | bg-en | 12,349 | 86.0% | 75.6% | 81.6% |
  | | bg-ru | | 45.9% | 42.3% | 57.9% |
  | | bg-en + bg-ru | | 87.1% | 77.1% | 84.0% |
  | UD Macedonian MTB test | mk-en | 1,104 | 98.6% | 94.6% | 95.3% |
  | | mk-ru | | 26.7% | 23.2% | 30.8% |

  Part of the gap to the ceiling is the treebank's lemmas (pronouns under аз, този), not lookup. Words found
  nothing although the lemma is a headword: Bulgarian 2.1% (nouns 1.4%: министерството, средства, конкуренцията;
  adjectives 0.6%: парламентарната, териториалната; verbs 0.1%: гарантира, функционират), Macedonian 0.5% (мојот,
  својот).
- An ending list (about 70 Bulgarian rules: articles -ът -ят -та -то -те -ите, plurals -и -ове -ища -ия, adjective
  forms with the fleeting vowel е/ъ restored, verbs -ам/-ирам/-вам/-я/-а forms) applied only to words found nothing:
  Bulgarian 1.5% right first, 0.3% wrong (often only by the treebank's lemma: постепенно → постепенен), 11% still nothing;
  Macedonian 0.5% right.
- Mongolian (no Cyrillic treebank: UD_Mongolian-GDUD is 88 sentences in Latin transliteration): on the Leipzig news
  sample (137,075 words) mn-en + mn-ru find 54.1%; stripping one of ~110 endings (case, plural, reflexive
  possessive, verb endings, each harmony variant) and looking the rest up as is or with -ах/-эх/-ох/-өх/-их/-х
  reaches 17.5% more; 28.4% stay. In a sample of 60 reached words about two thirds are the right lemma (асуухад →
  асуух, халдвараас → халдвар, байгуулна → байгуулах); false ones take a noun for a verb stem (хийнэ → хий "air", not
  хийх; барсан → бар "tiger", not барах). Frequent misses show the stem rules the list needs: ь before -ийн
  (хуулийн ← хууль), the fleeting vowel (тээврийн ← тээвэр, асуудлыг ← асуудал, хөгжлийн ← хөгжил), -нд/-ны after a
  long vowel (хугацаанд, хорооны); others are words the dictionaries lack (ийм, онцгой, шиг, дагуу, гаруй).
- ѝ (U+045D) and ѐ (U+0450) are single code points in NFC, but `HeadwordMarks` works on the decomposed form (и or е
  + U+0300): a grave in its marks would turn the Bulgarian pronoun ѝ "her" into the conjunction и and Macedonian сѐ
  "everything" into се. Their marks are the acute only.
- Standalone й stands for ѝ in OCR output and in much typed Bulgarian; й is no Macedonian letter at all.
- Bulgarian dictionaries and textbooks mark stress with a grave (кнѝга, NFC U+045D inside the word) as well as with
  an acute (кни́га).
- Macedonian letter retries (question 4): of the mk-en and mk-ru headwords with ќ, ѓ, љ, 312 of 11,417, 134 of 4,969
  and 292 of 4,113 have a twin spelled with к, г, л (mostly dialect or variant forms), so the retry runs only when
  the word as read finds nothing. Њ is read right, and н/њ twins are common (12,933 of 48,914), so it is not retried.

## Decisions this plan builds on

Rows of the languages plan: "Group 5" (bg, mn; mk added from the languages Yomitan lacks), "Group 5 research",
"Group 5 release" (after the big release, block by block), "Depth", "Catalog threshold" (the passing pairs above),
"Transcription threshold" (all three get one), "Own-language dictionaries" (none exists for these three),
"Recommended dictionaries", "Frequency dictionaries" (built on the phone from wordfreq: small_bg, large_mk), "Second
form-of step", "Inflection names and tags" (the ending lists' rule names in all 14 languages), "Rule chains", "Russian
stress" (the dictionary's stressed form shown, Anki's `{expression}` without marks, as for Ukrainian and Belarusian),
"On-device OCR engines" (models measured as unusable are not offered), "OCR engine choice" (the recommendation rule),
"Tesseract models", "Paddle model hosting", "OCR model list", "Latin look-alikes in Cyrillic text", "Serbian and
Croatian" (the model for a language's own look-alikes after recognition: Serbian j → ј), "Flags", "Flag country" (a
default flag only: Bulgaria, North Macedonia, Mongolia), "Bubble letters", "Audio order, groups 2 and 3" (by measured
coverage), "Text to speech" (last source), "Audio collections", "Font lists across languages", "Ordinals with a
period" (does not apply: these ordinals take a hyphen). Row "Scan length" of `2026-10-19-group-4b.md`: 16 for these
languages.

## Questions

1. Answered: (a). Mongolian script. Mongolia writes Cyrillic; the traditional vertical script is used in Inner
   Mongolia and in design. wty mn-en has 2,577 traditional-script headwords, 1,864 of them only pointing to the
   Cyrillic word; no on-device model reads the script. (a, recommended) Cyrillic only; (b) also the traditional
   script, read by the cloud, with vertical lines and a font of its own, as a separate piece after the rest.
2. Answered: (a). Bulgarian and Macedonian endings. The article is written onto the word (книгата, градът); a form is
   found only when Wiktionary lists it. On UD Bulgarian BTB (12,349 words): 87% find something, 77% the right
   lemma; an ending list adds 1.5% right lemmas and 0.3% wrong ones. Macedonian finds 98.6% (UD MTB); a list would
   add 0.5%. (a, recommended) A list for Bulgarian, none for Macedonian; (b) form tables only; (c) lists for both.
3. Answered: (a). Mongolian endings. Case and verb endings follow vowel harmony (номын, номоос, гэрээс, уншдаг);
   the dictionaries are small and few words have form tables. On 137k words of news text 54% are found; an ending
   list reaches 17.5% more, about two thirds of them the right lemma in a sample of 60, the rest false matches shown
   with their dropped ending. (a, recommended) Our own list; (b) form tables only.
4. Answered: (a). Macedonian letters on the phone. PaddleOCR reads S, Ґ, ё/è, й for Ѕ, Ѓ, ѐ, ѝ (fixed after
   recognition) and К, Л, Г for Ќ, Љ, Ѓ (real letters, not fixable blindly; ќе is among the most common words). The
   cloud reads them right. (a, recommended) A word with no entry is tried with Ќ, Љ, Ѓ for К, Л, Г, one at a time;
   (b) no retry.
5. Answered: (a). Bubble letters. Taken: Russian Я, Ukrainian Ї, Belarusian Ў, Serbian Ћ, Kazakh Қ, Kyrgyz Ң.
   Bulgarian has no letter of its own, Ъ is a frequent vowel there; Macedonian has Ѓ Ќ Ѕ (Ѕ looks like Latin S, Ќ
   like Kazakh Қ); Mongolian Ө Ү (Ү looks like Y). (a, recommended) Ъ, Ѓ, Ө; (b) Щ, Ќ, Ү; (c) БГ, МК, МН, two letters
   as Indonesian and Malay.
6. Answered: (a). Mongolian WordNet. Wiktionary gives mn-en 4.4k and mn-ru 3.2k words, 54% of news words found.
   MonWN has 23k lemmas on English synsets; a built mn→en finds 7.8% more words at once. Needs a build script and a
   place to host the file, not yet settled (as for the Paddle models). (a, recommended) Build it, hosted with our
   other files, Wiktionary only until then; (b) Wiktionary only.
7. Answered: (a). Order. Bulgarian: the largest dictionaries, stress marks and an ending list; Macedonian: a large
   mk-en with full form tables, letter fixes and the retry; Mongolian: the smallest dictionaries and the largest
   ending list, no recognition errors. (a, recommended) All three, bg, mk, mn; (b) bg and mk, mn after MonWN and
   hosting; (c) mn, bg, mk.
8. Answered: (a). Bulgarian letter forms. Bulgarian print often uses its own lowercase forms (д like g, т like m,
   и like u, triangular Д Л); the Russian forms are familiar too. The system font and the catalog's Cyrillic fonts
   have none, and PT lacks ѝ. (a, recommended) Montserrat, Commissioner and Sofia Sans, the system font by default;
   (b) Montserrat and Commissioner only; (c) nothing added.
9. Answered: (a). Macedonian voice. Recordings come first (Lingua Libre 19 of 20 words); the app speaks only with the
   default engine's voice of the language. Google's engine has no Macedonian, eSpeak NG has (installed separately,
   robotic). (a, recommended) As now; (b) look for the voice in every installed engine, for all languages; (c) the
   Bulgarian voice reads Macedonian.
10. Answered: (a). Mongolian audio. Lingua Libre has 17 words, Wiktionary 3, no engine a voice; the button would
    almost always say "No audio". (a, recommended) The button stays; (b) hidden for Mongolian, with its Anki audio.
11. Answered: (a). Mongolian frequency. wordfreq has no Mongolian list; Leipzig's news and Wikipedia corpora (100k
    sentences each, CC BY) have word counts. (a, recommended) Built on the PC and hosted with the MonWN dictionary;
    (b) built on the phone from the 19 MB archive; (c) none.
