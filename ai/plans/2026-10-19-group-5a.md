# Group 5a: Latin-script languages (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage F). That plan's decision rows bind here;
the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there. This file holds the
block's research, decisions, questions and progress (row "Group 5 research"); question numbers are its own.

## Goal

Eleven Latin-script languages, each looked up, read by OCR, voiced and exported as the group 3 and 4a languages are:

- Swedish (sv), Norwegian (no, written Bokmål) and Danish (da): Yomitan's descriptors (capitalization), Wiktionary's
  form tables, compounds split as German and Dutch with their own linking letters.
- Romanian (ro): Yomitan's descriptor (capitalization, diacritics removal) with our own ending list; the cedilla
  letters ş ţ read as ș ț; a word typed without diacritics also finds the words with them.
- Latin (la): Yomitan's descriptor (diacritics removal, æ and œ, 8 rules); macrons and breves dropped from lemma names
  at import; the enclitics -que, -ne, -ve; u read as v (uita finds vita).
- Lithuanian (lt), Slovak (sk), Slovenian (sl): no Yomitan descriptor; built from Latvian's and Czech's under their own
  codes; the stress marks of Lithuanian and Slovenian forms dropped at import; a word written without its letters'
  marks also finds the words with them; a Slovak word without a Slovak entry is looked up in the user's Czech
  dictionaries.
- Esperanto (eo): Yomitan's descriptor (24 rules) and the x-system (cx for ĉ); a word written without hats also finds
  the words with them.
- Albanian (sq): Yomitan's descriptor (94 rules) and our own ending list; the elided s' and ç'.
- Swahili (sw): no descriptor; built from Czech's with our own list of verb prefixes.

Basque (eu) and Maltese (mt), the other two languages of row "Group 5" for this block, are researched below and not
built (question 4).

- Marks in headwords and lemma names (NFD):
  - Lithuanian: lt-en lemma rows carry no accent; 114,096 of 190,940 form rows do (acute U+0301, grave U+0300, tilde
    U+0303 on vowels and on l m n r: angoliẽtis, Angòlos), and lemma names carry them too (Burùndis, abãtas). The soft
    dot of an accented i (i + U+0307, Hai̇̀tis, bi̇̀tė) appears in about 18k headwords; ė is e + U+0307 itself.
  - Slovenian: sl-en form rows carry tonemic marks (acute 28,480 rows, inverted breve U+0311 7,153: ravȃn; dot below
    U+0323 3,749: njẹ̑; circumflex 3,151: ôna; grave 1,716; double grave: ȁ ȅ); lemma rows and most lemma names do not
    (504 lemma names with an acute, 201 with a dot below, 164 with an inverted breve). ć appears as a letter (U+0301
    after c) in loanwords. sl-ru has 73 headwords with an acute (Argentína, ponedéljek).
  - Latin: la-en headwords are written without macrons, their readings with them; 780k form rows name their lemma
    with macrons (amō). Only 51.3% of the lemma names resolve to a headword as written, 80.6% with macrons (U+0304)
    and breves (U+0306) dropped from a, e, i, o, u, y.
  - Romanian: headwords use the comma letters (ș in 10,179 lemma headwords, ț in 9,591); the cedilla letters ş ţ
    appear in 20 ro-ru lemma rows and in no form row.
  - Slovak: acute, caron, circumflex (ô) and diaeresis (ä) are letters; no stress marks. Esperanto: ĉ ĝ ĥ ĵ ŝ ŭ, no
    headword in the x-system (cx). Maltese: ċ ġ ż (U+0307) and ħ.
- `HeadwordMarks` works on NFD text and drops a listed mark after a listed base letter. For Lithuanian the dot U+0307
  cannot be a listed mark (ė would become e); the soft dot is a replacement "i" + U+0307 → "i" instead. With acute,
  grave and tilde on aeiouylmnr and that replacement, lt-en lemma names resolve 70.9% → 71.6% (the rest name lemmas
  missing from the dictionary), and the top 1,000 wordfreq words found rise from 53.2% to 87.6% (form headwords keyed
  without marks).
- Norwegian: of the lemmas of nb-en and nn-en, 13,275 are in both, 9,059 only in nb-en, 7,512 only in nn-en; of
  no-ru's 8,051 lemmas, 4,983 are nb-en lemmas and 3,673 nn-en ones. nn-en finds 60.8% of the top 5,000 Bokmål
  words, nb-en 90.1%.
- Swahili: plural nouns are form rows (watoto → mtoto, vitabu → kitabu); present, habitual, infinitive, subjunctive
  and negative-present verb forms are rows, past (alisoma), future and perfect forms are not. Some rows are template
  residue (a subject prefix + "-likufa"). Headwords write the apostrophe as ' (ng'ombe).
- Maltese: headwords exist for "il-", "tal-", "bil-" and other article and preposition forms; apostrophes are mixed
  (' 173 rows, ’ 593: b’, f’, x’, ta’).

#### Albanian ending list (2026-10-20)

Measured for question 7 (the owner: keep the list only if its false hits are removed or cut to a few percent).

- Text: lower-case words of three samples of Albanian Wikipedia, about 17,000 words each: the four articles above
  (first set), Durrësi, Kosova, Ismail Kadare, Uji, Shkodra (second set) and Lufta e Dytë Botërore, Futbolli,
  Bujqësia, Mjekësia, Vlora, Ekonomia, Muzika (third set). The rules were tuned on the first two sets; the third was
  fetched and checked once, after the rules were fixed.
- Only words that neither a headword nor Yomitan's rules find are tried (about 20% of the words).
- Dictionary data: the rows of sq-en and sq-ru with their parts of speech (14,971 lemma headwords, 199k form
  headwords).
- Each rule names the part of speech of the entry it must reach:
  - adjectives: -e → -, -e → -ë, -a → -ë, -ta → -të, -ra → -ër, -la → -ël, -me → -ëm, -shme → -shëm;
  - verbs: -het -hen -hej → -j, -ohet -ohen -ohej → -oj, -et -en → -, -ua -uar -uara → -oj or -uaj, -uan → -oj, -ur
    -ura → -, -ye -yer → -ej, -on -oi -onte -ojnë → -oj, -jnë → -j;
  - nouns: -i -u -it -in -ut -un -et -ët -ve -eve → -, -ia -isë → -i, -ja → -je or -e, -es -en → -e, -ës -ën → -ë.
- The entry is reached as a lemma row of that part of speech, or as a form row whose lemma has it. A noun reached
  only through a form row must not itself end in a case ending (partneritet is not partnerit + -et).
- Longest ending first; at equal length nouns, then adjectives, then verbs (lejen → leje before lej); the first rule
  that reaches an entry gives the result. The stem keeps at least two letters (bëhej → bëj, hyjnë → hyj).
- Dropped while tuning: the list without parts of speech, one-letter endings without a condition (-t, -n), noun -ës →
  - (kronikës → kronik, stepës → step). A three-letter stem minimum removed as many right hits as false ones.
- False hits were marked by the agent from the dictionary data, without a native speaker; doubtful ones count as
  false.

### Text

- Word starts (row 35 of `notes/language-rules.md`): `'’-` between letters stay inside a word. Needed besides:
  - Romanian: a hyphen after a proclitic (s-, l-, m-, n-, v-, i-, într-, dintr-, printr-, de-, ne-) starts a new word,
    so "s-a" looks up "a" when aimed at it; enclitic forms (dă-mi, spune-mi) are found by the prefix "dă".
  - Albanian: s' (negation) and ç' (what) are elisions: the word after the apostrophe starts a new word, as French l'.
  - Maltese (not built): article and preposition forms (il-, l-, tal-, bil-, fil-, mill-, għall-, ir-, is-, it-) start
    a new word after their hyphen; in examples il 311 times, l 205, tal 95, bil 59, it 43, is 36, mill 34.
- Apostrophes: Swahili ng'ombe, Albanian s'kam, Romanian (rare) and Esperanto's elided -o (l', dom') are written
  with ' in the dictionaries and with ’ in typeset text; Ukrainian's processor (' ‘ ’ ʼ ` ´ each also tried as ', ’
  and ʼ) covers them.
