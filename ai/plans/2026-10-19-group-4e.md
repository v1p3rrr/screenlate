# Group 4e: Kazakh, Kyrgyz (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage E, question 30). That plan's decision
rows bind here; the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there.
Research: `notes/languages.md`, "Group 4e research".

## Goal

Two Turkic languages written in Cyrillic, each looked up with its endings, read by OCR, voiced and exported as the
other spaced languages are:

- Kazakh: Paddle's `cyrillic` recognizer (recommended), Tesseract `kaz` offered; Wiktionary into English and Russian
  (and French, Polish, Chinese, Korean), our own ending rules, the Kazakh letters OCR loses matched, Latin i read as
  і; a frequency dictionary from the Leipzig corpora; Lingua Libre recordings, a voice only from an engine the user
  installs.
- Kyrgyz: the same recognizer and Tesseract `kir`; Wiktionary into English and Russian (small) plus a converted PanLex
  dictionary once our hosting exists; the same rules in four-way vowel harmony; Lingua Libre recordings.

Technical choices of the agent (the owner may object; recorded in the changelog when changed):

| Topic | Choice |
|---|---|
| Branches | One per language, chained from `languages/main` in the order Kazakh, Kyrgyz; `languages/main` fast-forwarded after each, the ai/ changes copied to `main`. The shared pieces are built on the Kazakh branch. |
| Bundles | kk and ky borrowed from Mongolian's descriptor (Cyrillic, capitalization only) under their own codes (`kaz`, `kir`), as Bengali's is from Hindi's; our text processors through `addedPreprocessors` and our ending rules through `addedTransforms` (`own-transforms.js`). |
| Ending rules | Written from grammar descriptions (no GPL code). Noun: stem [plural] [possessive] [case] [predicative]; verb: stem [voice] [negation], then one of past + person, present + person, participle (+ possessive, case, predicative), converb, conditional + person, imperative; Kyrgyz also the attached question particle (-бы, -би, -бу, -бү, -пы …). Kazakh endings in their two harmony variants, Kyrgyz in four, with the consonant variants. The stem is tried as written and with the consonant change undone (аумағ- → аумақ, китеб- → китеп); a verb stem becomes the dictionary form (Kazakh оқы- → оқу, қой- → қою; Kyrgyz жаша- → жашоо, иште- → иштөө). The word as written comes first; then the spellings found as written before those reached through the letter keys, fewer endings first, then the more common words (the engine's order: match length, steps, keyed or not, chain length, the word itself, frequency). |
| Rule conditions | wty's rules field is empty and its part-of-speech tag (`n`, `v`, `adj`) sits in the definition tags, so noun chains match any entry (as Bengali's `untypedRules`) and verb chains only entries tagged `v`; the precision measured (36 and 37 of 40) assumed this check. |
| Letters OCR loses | Headwords also keyed at import with the Kazakh letters as their Russian twins (қ к, ғ г, ң н, ұ ү у, ө о, ә а, і и, һ х; Kyrgyz ң н, ө о, ү у), as the Greek tonos; the looked-up text is not keyed, so text in the twins finds the Kazakh words ("он": он, оң, өң) and a Kazakh letter written in it finds only its spelling ("оң": оң) (questions 3 and 11); the exact spelling first. A word that keeps some Kazakh letters and loses others (Қазакстан) matches no key: when the text as written finds nothing as long as its first word, it is looked up again keyed, and the longer matches whose headword has the letters the text writes are shown first (row "Written Kazakh letters": Russian letters in the same word may still stand for Kazakh ones). ы is not matched to і (question 12). |
| Look-alikes | Kazakh: Latin i I ə Ə h inside a Cyrillic word also looked up as і І ә Ә һ in any text (question 2), as a text processor of the bundle beside the letter keys, and in `ocrWord` as well, so recognized text and a note's sentence carry the Kazakh letters: i I h are typing habits (39-60% of those letters in the news list), ə Ə are not (0.06%) but stay in any text too (question 13: a Latin schwa never belongs in a Cyrillic word); the other Latin look-alikes of the row in OCR results only (`ocrWord`), as for every Cyrillic language; Kyrgyz: the row's rule plus ɵ Ɵ as ө Ө, in `ocrWord` only (0.04% typed). The Kazakh and Kyrgyz letters typed as their Russian twins (row "Letters OCR loses") are a typing habit too and stay for any text (row "Look-alike fixes only after OCR", refined 2026-10-20). |
| Form rows | Rows linking a Cyrillic headword to another script left out (tags `Arabic`, `romanization`, `Yañalif`; Arabic-script headwords pointing to Cyrillic stay, as only Arabic text reaches them), letter equivalents (о → و) dropped, as the Jawi and Baybayin rows (`ignoredFormTags`). |
| Hyphens | A hyphen after digits or an abbreviation (two capitals or more: АҚШ-қа, ҚазМҰУ-да; a unit: км-ге) ends that word (2007-жылы, 1990-ға): the lookup starts after it, and the ending alone is also tried with its hyphen, which finds wty's suffix entries (-ға; 312 Kazakh and 178 Kyrgyz suffix headwords). The rule of question 8 for the parts of a hyphenated word is built for every spaced language, with tests of the existing ones. |
| Sentence ends | Abbreviation lists of both languages (Kazakh т.б., т.с.с., ж., жж., ғ., ғғ., б.з.д., б.з.б., мыс., млн., млрд., қ., шам., көш., обл., ауд., ағыл., орыс.; Kyrgyz ж. б., б. з. ч., ж., жж., к., кк., б.а., мис., проф., обл., түн., түш.); a period after one before a lowercase letter or a digit ends nothing. Quotes «» and „“, as in Russian (“ closes the inner quotes, so it cannot open them as well). |
| Fonts | kk and ky take the catalog's Cyrillic fonts with `fontSubsets` latin, cyrillic, cyrillic-ext (Literata, Inter, PT Serif, PT Sans have all the letters); the system fonts cover them. |
| OCR | `paddle-cyrillic` gains kk and ky and is recommended for both (Kazakh 2.2% errors over clean and hard against Tesseract's 22.8-24.0%); Tesseract `kaz` and `kir`, fast and best, offered unticked (clean screens 0.0%). Kyrgyz Tesseract is measured on the emulator. |
| Frequency | Built on the phone from Leipzig's `kaz_newscrawl_2016_100K` and `kir_newscrawl_2016_100K` (pinned URLs, SHA-256 checked): the archive is read as a stream and only `-words.txt` kept (its place in the archive varies); words lowercased, Kazakh Latin i read as і, counts merged, top ~100 K. |
| PanLex | Converter in `scripts/` from the CC0 mirror `cointegrated/panlex-meanings` (2024-03-01 dump, pinned revision): Kazakh and Kyrgyz expressions (`kaz-000`, `kir-000`) joined with English and Russian ones on their meaning; filters for proper nouns and stray translations chosen on a sample and checked by hand; files checked on the emulator from a local address and listed once hosting exists (row "Dictionaries we convert"). The license is checked again before hosting (the live site says CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). |
| Audio | Lingua Libre first for both (20 common words: Kazakh 4, Kyrgyz 17; Wiktionary files 0 and 1), items Q9252 and Q9255; text to speech last. |
| Voice | No Google voice for either; the popup's existing "No text-to-speech voice for this language is installed" stays; `docs/usage.md` says that some languages need another text-to-speech engine, without naming one. |
| Names, codes | Names as Android gives them (Chinese 柯尔克孜语 for Kyrgyz kept, as the Bengali row); e-ink codes KK, KY; flags kz, kg. |

## Questions

1. Answered: (a). Endings. Only form rows today: 59% of Kazakh and 41% of Kyrgyz running text found. (a,
   recommended) Our own rules: 91% and 63%, right on 36-37 of 40. (b) The same plus the longest start: +3 and +13
   points, right on about 15 of 40. (c) Form rows only, as Turkish today.
2. Answered: (a). Latin i in Kazakh: 10% of the Cyrillic words of the news sample. (a, recommended) Read as і in any
   text, with I ə h; Latin words untouched. (b) Only in recognized words, as the other Cyrillic languages.
3. Answered: (a), asked again in plainer words after the owner proposed the same ("он" shows он and оң). The letters
   OCR and a Russian keyboard lose: 24 of the 32 misread words of the test screens are found when they are taken as
   their Russian twins; 0.9% of Kazakh and 0.7% of Kyrgyz dictionary words share such a key (он/оң/өң, күн/құн,
   сал/сәл). (a, recommended) Every match always, the exact spelling first. (b) Only when the word as written finds
   nothing. (c) Exact letters only.
4. Answered: (a). Bubble letters. (a, recommended) Қ and Ң; (b) Ә and Ө; (c) KK and KY.
5. Answered: (b). Kyrgyz dictionaries: ky-en 2.7k and ky-ru 3.7k words; 63% of running text found with the rules;
   common words missing (боюнча, чейин, же). (a, recommended) Wiktionary only. (b) Plus a PanLex converter (8.6k
   Kyrgyz words with a Russian translation, some wrong, license unclear), listed once hosting exists. (c) Defer
   Kyrgyz as Lao.
6. Answered: (a). Frequency: wordfreq has neither language. (a, recommended) Leipzig 100 K sentences (18.7 and 19.9
   MB archives, about 115k and 101k word forms, CC BY for downloads). (b) Leipzig 300 K (57 and 60 MB). (c) None.
7. Answered: (a). Kazakh Latin: four incompatible conventions (the 2018 decree with acutes, the 2021 drafts, the
   Turkic Academy's recommendation of June 2026, informal); no approved alphabet or date; about 1.5% of app reviews
   mostly Latin. (a, recommended) Cyrillic only until an alphabet is approved. (b) The 2018 alphabet converted to
   Cyrillic. (c) 2018 and 2026.
8. Answered: (a). Hyphenated words: 2-3% of the words; one in ten is a headword as a whole. Today a tap on the second
   part shows the first part when the whole is not a headword. (a, recommended) The whole word, then the tapped part,
   in every spaced language. (b) The same in Kazakh and Kyrgyz only. (c) As now.
9. Answered: (a). PanLex for Kazakh too: of the 2,000 most frequent news word forms Wiktionary finds 1,287, with PanLex
   1,405 (Kyrgyz 789 → 1,143). (a, recommended) Both languages, into English and Russian. (b) Kyrgyz only.
10. Answered: (a). PanLex on the download screen. (a, recommended) Ticked for Kyrgyz (an exception to "Recommended
    dictionaries"), unticked for Kazakh. (b) Unticked for both. (c) Ticked for both.
11. Answered: (a). A Kazakh letter already written. (a, recommended) Exact: "оң" finds only оң, as a Greek word with
    tonos. (b) Both ways: "оң" also shows он and өң.
12. Answered: (a). ы for і (the Android keyboard has і on the ы key): matching ы to і raises shared keys from 0.9% to
    1.5% (мына/мина, таныды/таниды). (a, recommended) No. (b) Only in the search field. (c) Everywhere.

## Questions for the owner (overnight)

Questions that come up during the build go here, marked deferred or decided provisionally, and are asked when the
owner is back.

13. Answered: (a) (owner, 2026-10-20). Latin ə in Kazakh and the rule "Look-alike fixes only after OCR" (owner,
    2026-10-20, refined the same day: matching made for OCR misreadings applies to OCR results only, text read from
    apps is looked up as it is, and matching for what people often type that way stays for any text). Question 2
    reads Kazakh Latin i, I, ə, h as і, І, ә, һ in any text. On the Leipzig news list Latin i is 39% of all i and і,
    I 44%, h 60% of h and һ: typing habits, so the refined rule keeps them for any text. Latin ə is 0.06% of ә: not a
    habit, so by the refined rule it would be for OCR results only. (a, provisional) As question 2 answered: ə Ə also
    in any text (a Latin schwa never belongs in a Cyrillic word, so it costs nothing). (b) ə Ə in `ocrWord` only, as
    the refined rule reads; changes the row "Latin i". Changing (a) to (b) moves two letters from the bundle's text
    processor to `ocrWord`, with their tests.
14. Answered (owner, 2026-10-21): (a). Not built yet. Particles written after a hyphen (сондай-ақ "also", келді-ау).
    Aimed at ақ in сондай-ақ, the popup shows ақ "white" and not wty's particle entry -ақ (wty-kk-en has -ақ and -ау as
    particles), since the part is looked up with its hyphen only after digits or an abbreviation. (a) In Kazakh and
    Kyrgyz, also look up every later part with its hyphen, before the part itself (сондай-ақ: -ақ, then ақ; ас-су: no
    "-су", so су as now); a suffix entry may then show for a part that is a word (-ма in a word written with a hyphen).
    (b) Leave it: the particle shows only when the hyphen is aimed at. Not built; (a) takes `takesHyphenedEnding` in
    `TurkicCyrillicSupport` returning true for any first part, with tests (about an hour).

15. Answered (owner, 2026-10-21): (b). Not built yet. A Kyrgyz or Kazakh word that keeps some of its letters and loses
    others is looked up again keyed only when the text as written finds less than the whole word. Түштук (түштүк "south"
    with its second ү typed у) is found as written: the past ending's twin spelling -тук (for -түк, kept for OCR and
    keyboards) makes it түшүү "to go down", "past « 1st pl.", and түштүк is not shown. (a) Leave it: the written reading
    is a real form when the letters are lost, and text with all its letters lost (туштук) finds both through the keys.
    (b) Also show the keyed results that match as much as the written ones, after them: one more lookup for every word
    that writes one of the language's letters; `keyedMatches` in `DictionaryLookup.kt` with `>=` in place of `>` and the
    order kept, with tests (about an hour). Not built.
16. Answered (owner, 2026-10-21): checked first: count on text how many wrong first results (b) removes and how many
    pronoun forms it loses; the numbers go to the owner, who then decides. A noun ending on a pronoun. алды ("took")
    shows ал "he/she/it" with "accusative" before алуу "to take" ("past"), since a nominal ending matches any entry and
    ал is more frequent; the pronoun's accusative is аны, so the first result is wrong. The same holds in Kazakh (алды,
    оны). (a) Leave it: both are shown, the verb second. (b) Let the noun endings skip entries tagged `pron`
    (Wiktionary's tag) in Kazakh and Kyrgyz: glue.js filters by the tag as it does for verbs; a pronoun's regular forms
    (менин, сенде) would then need form rows, which wty has for some; with glue and instrumented tests (about an hour
    and a half). Not built.
17. Answered (owner, 2026-10-21): (a), as built. How a PanLex entry looks. PanLex gives a word many meanings, each with
    its translations, one per source (Kyrgyz кол: a dozen meanings with only рука, others with ладонь, рука; кисть руки,
    рука; автограф, подпись, роспись). (a, provisional, built) One definition per headword: its translations
    comma-separated, the ones more meanings give first, at most ten (кол → "рука, кисть руки, ладонь, автограф, подпись,
    роспись", with stress marks); no part-of-speech tags, as PanLex has none here. (b) One definition per meaning, like
    a sense: shows which translations belong together (the "signature" sense apart from "hand"), but repeats рука in a
    dozen lines (merging meanings with the same translations leaves four lines for кол). Changing (a) to (b) changes
    `join` and `build` in `scripts/panlex/panlex_yomitan.py` and their tests (about an hour).
18. Answered (owner, 2026-10-21): checked first. The owner asked whether words would be lost: ordinary words are
    lowercase in PanLex and a capitalized word is also looked up lowercase, so only what PanLex writes capitalized is
    dropped. Count the capitalized headwords for kk and ky and review 50 at random (useful, already in wty, junk); the
    numbers go to the owner, who then decides between (a) and (b). Names in PanLex. Capitalized headwords are left out:
    in the sample they were mostly names, time zones and interface strings whose translation is the name itself (Бишкек
    → Бишкек), and Kyrgyz lists countries under unrelated meanings (кыргыз → Uzbekistan). (a, provisional, built)
    Lowercase headwords only. (b) Keep capitalized headwords whose translations are capitalized too (countries and
    cities with their Russian or English names). Changing it takes a filter in `is_headword` and a test (half an hour);
    wty has the common names already (Қазақстан).
