# Group 4e: Kazakh, Kyrgyz (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage E, question 30). That plan's decision
rows bind here; the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there.
Research: `notes/languages.md`, "Group 4e research".

## Goal

Two Turkic languages written in Cyrillic, each looked up with its endings, read by OCR, voiced and exported as the
other spaced languages are:

- Kazakh: Paddle's `cyrillic` recognizer (recommended), Tesseract `kaz` offered; Wiktionary into English and Russian
  (and French, Polish, Chinese, Korean), our own ending rules, the Kazakh letters OCR loses matched, Latin i read as
  і; a frequency dictionary from the Leipzig corpora; Lingua Libre recordings, a voice only from an engine the user
  installs.
- Kyrgyz: the same recognizer and Tesseract `kir`; Wiktionary into English and Russian (small) plus a converted PanLex
  dictionary once our hosting exists; the same rules in four-way vowel harmony; Lingua Libre recordings.

Technical choices of the agent (the owner may object; recorded in the changelog when changed):

| Topic | Choice |
|---|---|
| Branches | One per language, chained from `languages/main` in the order Kazakh, Kyrgyz; `languages/main` fast-forwarded after each, the ai/ changes copied to `main`. The shared pieces are built on the Kazakh branch. |
| Bundles | kk and ky borrowed from Mongolian's descriptor (Cyrillic, capitalization only) under their own codes (`kaz`, `kir`), as Bengali's is from Hindi's; our text processors through `addedPreprocessors` and our ending rules through `addedTransforms` (`own-transforms.js`). |
| Ending rules | Written from grammar descriptions (no GPL code). Noun: stem [plural] [possessive] [case] [predicative]; verb: stem [voice] [negation], then one of past + person, present + person, participle (+ possessive, case, predicative), converb, conditional + person, imperative; Kyrgyz also the attached question particle (-бы, -би, -бу, -бү, -пы …). Kazakh endings in their two harmony variants, Kyrgyz in four, with the consonant variants. The stem is tried as written and with the consonant change undone (аумағ- → аумақ, китеб- → китеп); a verb stem becomes the dictionary form (Kazakh оқы- → оқу, қой- → қою; Kyrgyz жаша- → жашоо, иште- → иштөө). The word as written comes first; then the spellings found as written before those reached through the letter keys, fewer endings first, then the more common words (the engine's order: match length, steps, keyed or not, chain length, the word itself, frequency). |
| Rule conditions | wty's rules field is empty and its part-of-speech tag (`n`, `v`, `adj`) sits in the definition tags, so noun chains match any entry (as Bengali's `untypedRules`) and verb chains only entries tagged `v`; the precision measured (36 and 37 of 40) assumed this check. |
| Letters OCR loses | Headwords also keyed at import with the Kazakh letters as their Russian twins (қ к, ғ г, ң н, ұ ү у, ө о, ә а, і и, һ х; Kyrgyz ң н, ө о, ү у), as the Greek tonos; the looked-up text is not keyed, so text in the twins finds the Kazakh words ("он": он, оң, өң) and a Kazakh letter written in it finds only its spelling ("оң": оң) (questions 3 and 11); the exact spelling first. A word that keeps some Kazakh letters and loses others (Қазакстан) matches no key: when the text as written finds nothing as long as its first word, it is looked up again keyed, and the longer matches whose headword has the letters the text writes are shown first (row "Written Kazakh letters": Russian letters in the same word may still stand for Kazakh ones). ы is not matched to і (question 12). |
| Look-alikes | Kazakh: Latin i I ə Ə h inside a Cyrillic word also looked up as і І ә Ә һ in any text (question 2), as a text processor of the bundle beside the letter keys, and in `ocrWord` as well, so recognized text and a note's sentence carry the Kazakh letters: i I h are typing habits (39-60% of those letters in the news list), ə Ə are not (0.06%) but stay in any text too (question 13: a Latin schwa never belongs in a Cyrillic word); the other Latin look-alikes of the row in OCR results only (`ocrWord`), as for every Cyrillic language; Kyrgyz: the row's rule plus ɵ Ɵ as ө Ө, in `ocrWord` only (0.04% typed). The Kazakh and Kyrgyz letters typed as their Russian twins (row "Letters OCR loses") are a typing habit too and stay for any text (row "Look-alike fixes only after OCR", refined 2026-10-20). |
| Form rows | Rows linking a Cyrillic headword to another script left out (tags `Arabic`, `romanization`, `Yañalif`; Arabic-script headwords pointing to Cyrillic stay, as only Arabic text reaches them), letter equivalents (о → و) dropped, as the Jawi and Baybayin rows (`ignoredFormTags`). |
| Hyphens | A hyphen after digits or an abbreviation (two capitals or more: АҚШ-қа, ҚазМҰУ-да; a unit: км-ге) ends that word (2007-жылы, 1990-ға): the lookup starts after it, and the ending alone is also tried with its hyphen, which finds wty's suffix entries (-ға; 312 Kazakh and 178 Kyrgyz suffix headwords). The rule of question 8 for the parts of a hyphenated word is built for every spaced language, with tests of the existing ones. |
| Sentence ends | Abbreviation lists of both languages (Kazakh т.б., т.с.с., ж., жж., ғ., ғғ., б.з.д., б.з.б., мыс., млн., млрд., қ., шам., көш., обл., ауд., ағыл., орыс.; Kyrgyz ж. б., б. з. ч., ж., жж., к., кк., б.а., мис., проф., обл., түн., түш.); a period after one before a lowercase letter or a digit ends nothing. Quotes «» and „“, as in Russian (“ closes the inner quotes, so it cannot open them as well). |
| Fonts | kk and ky take the catalog's Cyrillic fonts with `fontSubsets` latin, cyrillic, cyrillic-ext (Literata, Inter, PT Serif, PT Sans have all the letters); the system fonts cover them. |
| OCR | `paddle-cyrillic` gains kk and ky and is recommended for both (Kazakh 2.2% errors over clean and hard against Tesseract's 22.8-24.0%); Tesseract `kaz` and `kir`, fast and best, offered unticked (clean screens 0.0%). Kyrgyz Tesseract is measured on the emulator. |
| Frequency | Built on the phone from Leipzig's `kaz_newscrawl_2016_100K` and `kir_newscrawl_2016_100K` (pinned URLs, SHA-256 checked): the archive is read as a stream and only `-words.txt` kept (its place in the archive varies); words lowercased, Kazakh Latin i read as і, counts merged, top ~100 K. |
| PanLex | Converter in `scripts/` from the CC0 mirror `cointegrated/panlex-meanings` (2024-03-01 dump, pinned revision): Kazakh and Kyrgyz expressions (`kaz-000`, `kir-000`) joined with English and Russian ones on their meaning; filters for proper nouns and stray translations chosen on a sample and checked by hand; files checked on the emulator from a local address and listed once hosting exists (row "Dictionaries we convert"). License rechecked on 2026-10-22: PanLex's license page released its snapshots under CC0 until at least May 2025 and says CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 since, so the pinned 2024-03-01 dump stays CC0 and no newer PanLex data is used (`notes/languages.md`, "PanLex license history"). |
| Audio | Lingua Libre first for both (20 common words: Kazakh 4, Kyrgyz 17; Wiktionary files 0 and 1), items Q9252 and Q9255; text to speech last. |
| Voice | No Google voice for either; the popup's existing "No text-to-speech voice for this language is installed" stays; `docs/usage.md` says that some languages need another text-to-speech engine, without naming one. |
| Names, codes | Names as Android gives them (Chinese 柯尔克孜语 for Kyrgyz kept, as the Bengali row); e-ink codes KK, KY; flags kz, kg. |
