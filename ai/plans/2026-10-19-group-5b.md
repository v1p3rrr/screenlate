# Group 5b: Welsh, Irish, Scottish Gaelic (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage F). That plan's decision rows bind here;
the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there. This file holds the
block's research, decisions, questions and progress (row "Group 5 research"); question numbers are its own.

## Goal

Three Celtic languages, written in the Latin script with spaces between words. Their words change their first letters
with the grammar (initial mutations: Welsh bara → ei fara, Irish bean → an bhean, Gaillimh → i nGaillimh), so the
lookup undoes a mutation with a rule of our own before it looks the word up. Each language is looked up, read by OCR,
voiced and exported as the group 4a languages are:

- Welsh (cy): wty cy-en, cy-fr, cy-ru and a transcription; the mutation rule; Wiktionary and Lingua Libre recordings,
  Google's voice.
- Irish (ga): wty ga-en, ga-ru, ga-fr, ga-pl and a transcription; the mutation rule in place of Yomitan's noun
  eclipsis; elided d' m' b' looked up as French l' is; Lingua Libre recordings.
- Scottish Gaelic (gd): wty gd-en, gd-ru and a transcription; the mutation rule; elided dh' m' d' b'; no recordings
  of common words and no voice.

Breton (br) was researched with them and dropped from the group (owner, 2026-10-20, question 5): wty, which supplies
every other Wiktionary dictionary, has none for it, and the other Breton dictionaries are French or Dutch under the
GPL or not downloadable. Its measurements stay in the Research section for reference.

### Yomitan at e7334a9

| lang | ISO 639-3 | text processors | transforms |
|---|---|---|---|
| cy | cym | capitalization | none |
| ga | gle | capitalization | `irishTransforms`: one transform, "eclipsis", 7 prefix rules (mb gc nd bhf ng bp dt → b c d f g p t), nouns only (conditions `n` → `n`) |
| gd | gla | capitalization | none |
| br | bre | capitalization | none (added in Yomitan PR 2469, 2026-07-29) |

- So a mutated word is found today only through the dictionaries' form-of rows, plus Irish noun eclipsis.
- Yomitan's eclipsis misses capitalized radicals: nGaeilge decapitalizes to ngaeilge → gaeilge, which misses the
  headword Gaeilge. Its rules take and give `n`, so they can chain (mbmbean).
- `build.mjs` already has `addedTransforms` (own-transforms.js, Bengali's endings) for languages Yomitan has none for,
  and fails when Yomitan gains transforms for such a language; glue.js `untypedRules` lets a language's rule results
  match entries without a part of speech. Irish needs a way to replace Yomitan's transforms.

### Initial mutations: the grammar

The reverse rule gives, for a word in the text, the radical forms it may come from; the dictionary filters them.

| Language | Mutation | Radical → mutated | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| cy | soft | p→b, t→d, c→g, b→f, d→dd, g→(dropped), m→f, ll→l, rh→r | bara → (ei) fara, gwely → wely, Caerdydd → (o) Gaerdydd |
| cy | nasal | p→mh, t→nh, c→ngh, b→m, d→n, g→ng | Cymru → (yng) Nghymru, tad → (fy) nhad |
| cy | aspirate | p→ph, t→th, c→ch | caws → (a) chaws |
| cy | h-prothesis | vowel → h+vowel | wyneb → (ei) hwyneb |
| ga | lenition | b c d f g m p s t → bh ch dh fh gh mh ph sh th | bean → (an) bhean, fág → d'fhág |
| ga | eclipsis | b→mb, c→gc, d→nd, f→bhf, g→ng, p→bp, t→dt, vowel → n-vowel; before a capital nG, bhF, nA | Gaillimh → (i) nGaillimh, athair → (ár) n-athair |
| ga | t-prothesis | vowel → t-vowel, s → ts; before a capital tA, tS | uisce → (an) t-uisce, sráid → (an) tsráid |
| ga | h-prothesis | vowel → h+vowel | Éire → (na) hÉireann |
| gd | lenition | as Irish (l n r unwritten) | grian → (a') ghrian |
| gd | prefixes | h-, t-, n- before vowels with a hyphen, before capitals too; t-s | òrain → (na) h-òrain, Alba → (na) h-Alba |
| br | soft | p→b, t→d, k→g, b→v, d→z, g→c'h, gw→w, m→v | tad → (e) dad, mamm → (ur) vamm |
| br | aspirate | p→f, t→z, k→c'h | tad → (va) zad, kazh → (ar) c'hazh |
| br | hard | b→p, d→t, g→k, gw→kw | |
| br | mixed | b→v, d→t, g→c'h, gw→w, m→v | |
| br | nasal | d→n after the article | dor → (an) nor |

The reverse rule is as ambiguous as the grammar: Welsh f is soft b or m, a word starting with a vowel, w, l or r may
have lost a g, l may be ll, r may be rh; Breton v is b or m, z is d or t, c'h is g or k.

### Wiktionary's mutated forms

- Form-of rows in the English editions: cy-en 20,826 (tags `soft` 12,033, `prothesis-h` 3,882, `soft mutation` 2,367,
  `nasal mutation` 1,506, `aspirate mutation` 1,019); ga-en 39,922 (`eclipsis` 19,815, `lenition` 17,867, `lenited
  form` 1,962, `prothesis-h` 272); gd-en 5,506 (`lenition mutation` 3,169; `mutation prothesis-t`, `mutation
  prothesis-h`, `eclipsis mutation` 594 each, the last being n-; `lenited form` 437; `mutation mutation-radical`
  104). cy-fr 5,004 (`lenition`, `nasalization`, `unmutated`). The ru, pl and other editions have almost none.
- Share of words with their own meaning whose mutated forms are listed: cy-en 70.6%, ga-en 76.4%, gd-en 26.4%, cy-fr
  38.1%.
- Bad rows: cy-en has 2,348 `soft` rows whose lemma is a letter or an English word (fara → b, ferch → m, din → thin);
  ga-en's 272 `prothesis-h` rows are reversed (iomlán → hiomlán), so are gd-en's 104 `mutation-radical` rows (cill →
  chill); gd-en has 293 `lenition mutation` rows whose form is a note (`after "an"` → sir); cy-fr's `unmutated` and
  `lenition` rows point at wrong lemmas (lle → le).
- Parts of speech: ga-en carries them (n 14,148, adj 3,556, v 1,724, adv 322 of the first 22k rows; 2,665 none);
  cy and gd carry none.
- Breton in kaikki: the French edition lists 13,138 words with a mutation sense ("Forme mutée de …") and 60,303
  mutation-tagged forms in its tables (soft, spirant, hard); the English edition 58 mutation senses and 2,285
  table forms. French Wiktionary writes c'h with U+02BC (cʼhazh).

### The own rule against the dictionaries

- Rows the reverse rule explains (form → lemma): cy-en 87.0%, ga-en 99.3%, gd-en 92.0%; Breton singular mutation rows
  br-en 90.4%, br-fr (kaikki tables) 63.7%. The rest are the bad rows above and real cases outside the rule: Welsh
  fused forms (ogystal in "yn ogystal", "as well"), ŵ/ŷ changing with the mutation (cwyn → gŵyn), Gaelic n-/t-
  before capitals without a hyphen in table cells (tAlbannach), Breton comparatives and superlatives (vrasoc'h →
  bras) and suppletive plurals; kaikki's French tables also attach forms to wrong lemmas (bin → fin) and hold the word
  "inchangé" as a form.
- Extra entries for a word that is not mutated (the rule reaches another headword): cy-en 1.7% of headwords (dant
  "tooth" → tant "string", nant "stream" → dant), ga-en 0.5% (chan → can), gd-en 0.3%; another 0.6% of Welsh
  headwords would reach a mutated form row (afon → gafon → cafon), which a guard against a second mutation stops.
- A radical must keep at least two letters: without that, Gaelic th' and bh' (elided tha, bha) became t' and b', 600
  wrong hits in the sample.
- Candidates per word (extra lookups per substring): cy 0.9 (the dropped g makes one for every word starting with a
  vowel, w, l or r), br 0.5, ga 0.17, gd 0.17.

- 4-14% of running-text words are mutated forms. With the English editions the rule adds 1-2.5 points (the forms they
  do not list); with the small editions it adds 30-60% to what is found.
- Precision: of the 40 commonest words the rule gains in each text (en + ru), all Irish ones are right and one each in
  Welsh and Gaelic is wrong (right: anwyd → ganwyd, nghanol → canol, haghaidh → aghaidh, tAontas → Aontas, mhàthair →
  màthair; wrong: Welsh fed, an ordinal ending after digits, and English "the" in Gaelic text → te "hot").
- With a small dictionary alone the rule also reaches wrong entries for common words the dictionary lacks: Breton gant
  "with" → kant "hundred" (2,361 tokens) and bet "been" → pet with br-ru; Welsh mae "is" → bae "bay" with cy-fr;
  Welsh na "than, not" → da "good" with cy-ru. The popup shows the mutation's name with such an entry.
- Dropping the dictionaries' mutation rows altogether loses only a few fused forms (ogystal, 93 tokens in the Welsh
  text), so the rows stay and the rule's duplicates are removed at import (technical choices).
- Misses are mostly words the dictionaries lack (cy swyddogol, ymchwil; ga speiceas, daonáireamh, sna; gd ruige,
  fheudar), units (km, cm) and English words.

### Words the rule leaves alone (2026-10-20, question 9)

Candidates: the 1,000 commonest lowercase words of the same texts that have an entry in the language's English
dictionary and that the rule turns into another headword of any catalog dictionary of the language. Run with the
research's scripts on the samples above (the real list would come from a larger corpus; these texts are Wikipedia).

- What the dictionary tells: a candidate is either one whose rule result the English dictionary does not give as its
  lemma (mae → bae, ganwyd "was born" → canwyd "was sung", o "of" → go "rather"), or one it does, because Wiktionary
  lists every grammatical mutation, the rare ones too (na "than" as the nasal form of da "good", ar "on" as the soft
  form of gar "shin", alongside the common ones: bobl → pobl, ben → pen in "ar ben").
- Welsh: 58 candidates of the first kind and 215 of the second; of the second, 27 have a sense of their own and are at
  least 5 times as common as the rule's result (ar, gan, na, de, dŵr → tŵr, hen, dau, dal, môr). Those 85 words
  together: tokens shown an extra entry with en + ru from 34.6% to 14.5%; with cy-fr alone the rule's only hits from
  4.3% to 1.3% of tokens (i → gi, mae → bae, er → ger, nad → dad no longer found), with cy-ru alone na → da, dail
  "leaves" → tail "dung" no longer found. The frequency test is not enough by itself: it also takes lan (ar lan, of
  glan "bank"), which is a mutated form in most of its uses, so the list is reviewed by hand with the words before
  each candidate in the corpus (the script prints them).
- Irish and Gaelic: few candidates and most of them right as mutations: Irish bhí → bí (the past tense is lenited),
  hÉireann → Éireann (the English dictionary lacks that form row); Gaelic thug → tug, dha → da, chan "not" → can
  "say". The list there is a handful of words (Gaelic chan, dha), chosen by hand from the same print; the measured
  extra entries change little (Gaelic 6.8% → 6.0% of tokens).
- Cost: a listed word that is a mutated form in some text (Welsh dal "still" as the soft form of tal "tall") is found only
  through the dictionary's own mutation row, so the import cleanup keeps the rows of listed words; with a small
  dictionary alone such a reading is lost.

### Lookup start, highlight, elisions

- Word characters: ' ’ and - between letters stay inside a word (every spaced language), so n-athair, t-uisce,
  h-Alba, nGaillimh, c'hazh and mae'r are one word under the tap and the lookup starts at their first letter. The
  match keeps its length in the text (Yomitan maps the processed text back), so the highlight covers the whole
  mutated word: nGaillimh, n-athair, fara.
- Welsh clitics after an apostrophe (mae'r, i'r, chi'n, i'w): Yomitan's shrink at ' finds mae; nothing more is
  needed. Breton c'h / c’h is a letter, never an elision; Breton n' and d' (ne and da before a vowel: n'eo, d'ar)
  are.
- Irish and Gaelic elided words before an apostrophe: ga d' (past tense, de, do), m' (mo), b' (ba); gd dh', m', d',
  b'. In the Irish text 1,365 tokens start with one of them: 679 are headwords whole (d'éirigh, b'fhéidir), 594 more
  are found when the part after the apostrophe is looked up (d'aois, d'fhoilsigh, b'é, m'athair), 94 neither. Gaelic:
  737 tokens, 383 whole, 267 more after the apostrophe. Today the whole word is looked up and, failing that, shrunk
  to d' or m'. The Gaelic article and possessive a' (a' ghrian) stand apart and need nothing.
- Apostrophes: headwords mostly write ' (ga-en 13,566 with ', 121 with ’; cy-en 672 / 41; gd-en 938 / 6; cy-fr 0 /
  29); kaikki's French Breton writes ʼ (U+02BC); text writes ' and ’, so the three need to match each other.
- Gaelic older spelling: acute vowels (mór for mòr) are 5% of the accented vowels in the Wikipedia sample; gd-en has
  176 headwords with acute vowels against 10,265 with grave ones. Irish in Gaelic type writes lenition as a dot
  (ḃean); none in the Irish sample, 12 ga-en headwords.
- Scan length: 2.75% of cy-en's headwords (forms included) are longer than 16 characters, ga-en 9.6% (long verb and
  compound forms, mostly found through their shorter parts), gd-en 6.9%, br-fr 1.0%.

- Recordings are titled by the radical form; the search uses the entry's headword, so a mutated word in the text

- So Welsh has recordings for about half of common words plus Google's voice; Irish and Breton have recordings for
  most common words and no voice; Gaelic has neither.

## Questions

1. Answered: (a). Initial mutations. A word's first letters change with the grammar; today such a word is found only
   when Wiktionary lists the form (English editions 70-76% of Welsh and Irish words, 26% of Gaelic; the Russian ones
   almost none); Yomitan handles only Irish noun eclipsis. Text found with en + ru: cy 92.5% → 94.0%, ga 93.0% →
   94.0%, gd 89.9% → 91.7%; ru only: cy 12.3% → 17.1%, ga 12.7% → 16.5%, gd 7.3% → 9.6%. Cost: sometimes an extra
   entry (dant "tooth" also shows tant "string"; 1.7% of Welsh words, 0.5% of Irish). (a, recommended) Our rule, the
   mutation's name under the word, Wiktionary's rows kept without duplicates; (b) the rule without the name; (c) no
   rule.
2. Answered: (a). Irish and Gaelic words with an apostrophe (d'ól, m'athair, b'fhéidir). Today the whole word is
   looked up and, failing that, d' alone. Irish text: 1,365 such words, 679 found whole, 594 more by the part after
