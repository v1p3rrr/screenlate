# Group 4d: Arabic, Persian, Hebrew (plan of 2026-10-19)

Part of the languages work (`2026-10-10-languages-implementation.md`, Stage E, question 30). That plan's decision
rows bind here; the ones this plan builds on are listed below by name, and changes to them are recorded there.
Research: `notes/languages.md`, "Group 4d research". Questions are numbered after the languages plan's (69 on).

## Goal

Three languages written right to left with spaces between words, each looked up from the word under the tap, read by
OCR, voiced and exported as the other languages are, with the text in the popup, on the screen and in Anki in its
own direction while the interface stays left to right:

- Arabic: Paddle's Arabic recognizer once the owner releases the waiting models, Tesseract `ara` until then;
  Yomitan's Arabic rules; Wiktionary and Lingua Libre recordings, Google's voice.
- Persian: Paddle's Arabic recognizer once released; until then no on-device model, as Tesseract `fas` is measured
  unusable (row "On-device OCR engines"), so cloud recognition only; our own ending rules; Lingua Libre and
  Wiktionary recordings, no voice.
- Hebrew: Tesseract `heb`; our own prefix rules; Lingua Libre and Wiktionary recordings, Google's voice if the
  emulator confirms it.
- Urdu: later, on its own (row "Urdu"); group 5f of the languages plan holds it with Yiddish and Egyptian Arabic,
  whose findings are in the research section.

## Decisions this plan builds on

Rows of the languages plan: "Scope of the big release", "Depth", "On-device recognition" (right-to-left scripts are
supported for their languages), "Catalog threshold", "Transcription threshold", "Own-language dictionaries" (none
exist for these), "Recommended dictionaries", "Frequency dictionaries" (built on the phone), "Inflection names and
tags" (Arabic's 50 rule names and descriptions, about 1,300 strings over 13 locales; our Persian and Hebrew rules
too), "On-device OCR engines" (Tesseract is not offered for Persian), "OCR engine choice" (the recommendation rule),
"Tesseract models" (fast for Hebrew, best offered), "Paddle model hosting", "OCR model list", "Flags", "Flag country"
(a default flag only), "Bubble letters", "Audio order, groups 2 and 3" (by measured coverage), "Text to speech" (last
source), "Font lists across languages", "Greek accents" (the model for spellings matched at import), "Furigana
markers" (offered only for Japanese, Chinese and Russian, so not for these; `{reading}` holds the vowelled Arabic
word and the Persian Latin reading). Question 4 of `2026-10-19-group-4c.md` (row "Paddle models upload"): the Arabic
model goes up with the other waiting Paddle models, in one release, when the owner decides.

Technical choices of the agent (the owner may object; recorded in the changelog when changed):

|---|---|

| Shared right-to-left pieces | Built on the Arabic branch: `dir` on the popup's blocks of right-to-left text (row "Popup"); the Anki wrap (row "Anki fields"); app text lines in right-to-left text views; Paddle `readingOrder` fixes; cloud recognition's mixed lines ordered from word boxes; `OcrScript` ARABIC and HEBREW; `LanguageSupport.rightToLeft` used where direction is needed (or replaced by the script check, if nothing needs it). | Glue and bundles | Transforms registered by the descriptor's iso (fixes Georgian now, Egyptian Arabic later); empty deinflected texts dropped; `generate-inflections.mjs` by iso; `build.mjs` able to add processors to descriptors without the capitalization line (fa, he); our Persian and Hebrew rules as own transforms in their bundles, as Bengali's endings. |
| Arabic spellings | Headwords keyed at import with every hamza alef (أ إ آ ٱ) as ا, final ى as ي and final ة as ه (as Greek's accents); Yomitan's addHamzaTop and addHamzaBottom replaced (`replacedProcessors`), its final ى → ي kept. |
| Arabic adjectives | Candidates of the noun rules also fit entries without a part of speech (`untyped`, as English phrasal verbs): wty ar-en leaves 7,405 own rows (adjectives, adverbs) and 164,359 form rows untagged. |
| Arabic form-of | Lemma links compared without harakat (11,029 rows); `ar-infl-*` template names dropped from tags; romanized Latin form rows dropped. |
| Persian text | Text and keys without ZWNJ; ي ك ى → ی ک ی and ۀ → ه; the joined tries of row "Persian ZWNJ"; Cyrillic Tajik (6,192) and romanized form rows dropped; speech reads the term, not the Latin reading. |
| Hebrew text | Niqqud and cantillation removed as a text variant and in import keys (not the maqaf, paseq, sof pasuq, nun hafukha); a trailing maqaf dropped from keys; ASCII `'` `"` between Hebrew letters tried as ׳ ״; geresh, gershayim and those quotes as word characters; the maqaf as a word connector like the hyphen. No plene keys (they add 133 of the 20,000 commonest words). |
| OCR letters | `ocrWord` writes Persian ي ك ى as ی ک ی and Arabic ک ی as ك ي (Paddle with a Naskh font gives Arabic letters in Persian; cloud recognition once gave Persian ک in an Arabic word). |
| Sentence ends | ؟ for Arabic and Persian; ، and ؛ end nothing; Hebrew as now. |
| Frequency | wordfreq `large_ar`, `small_fa`, `large_he`; Persian keys with ي ك ى → ی ک ی and no ZWNJ, Hebrew ASCII quotes → ׳ ״. |
| Audio | Order by the row "Audio order": Arabic Wiktionary, Lingua Libre; Persian and Hebrew Lingua Libre, Wiktionary; text to speech last. The two Commons bugs (an error answer cached as no recording; a redirect's target dropped) fixed on the Arabic branch, for every language. |
| Catalog | Pairs ar en ru fr ko pl tr zh; fa en ru fr pl zh; he en ru es ja pl zh; transcriptions ar, fa, he; flags sa, ir, il; the fonts of row "Fonts"; tessdata ara and heb (not fas, row "On-device OCR engines"); Paddle arabic for ar and fa once released. |

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision |
|---|---|
| Urdu | Later, on its own, after Arabic, Persian and Hebrew, with a measurement of Nastaliq recognition; the shared right-to-left pieces will be ready by then (owner, 2026-10-19, question 69); the row "Group 5" of the languages plan puts it into block 5f |
| Interface languages | Arabic, Persian and Hebrew as interface languages with mirrored screens: yes, as a separate task after group 5's research (and maybe its build) and group 4's build; until then the 14 left-to-right interface languages stay and only the studied text runs right to left (owner, 2026-10-19, question 70) |
| Popup | The layout stays: the word, its vowelled form, the recognized sentence and examples in Arabic, Persian or Hebrew run right to left in their places; meanings in other languages as now (owner, 2026-10-19, question 71) |
| Anki fields | The `<span dir="rtl">…</span>` stands in the field templates of the app's Anki settings, around the markers of Arabic, Persian or Hebrew text (the word, the sentence and its cloze parts, Arabic's vowelled reading; not meanings, transcriptions or Persian's Latin reading), where the user sees and edits it: `<span dir="rtl">{cloze-prefix}<b>{cloze-body}</b>{cloze-suffix}</span>`. For these languages "Suggest templates" and the marker picker put it there automatically; no new control, the ⓘ of the fields explains it. The note type's first field gets none, so that AnkiDroid's duplicate check still finds notes added without it (owner, 2026-10-19: question 72, the wrap; question 73, not the first field, after the owner asked which is more correct and got a plainer explanation; question 86, in the template and added automatically: "this span can be added right into the field automatically, or by a button shown with some checkbox; and in the field itself, the template of how a field is added in Anki, this RTL tag is written, and inside it our marker", then "automatically" of the two) |
| Arabic spellings | A word without hamza matches headwords with hamza anywhere (اكبر → أكبر, اسلام → إسلام), final ى and ي match each other, a final ه matches ة; a word with hamza matches exactly. 14,601 of the 20,000 commonest words before prefixes and endings, against 13,847 exactly; 292 spellings belong to two headwords and both show (على, علي) (agent's choice at the owner's request, "I don't know Arabic, do what is best", 2026-10-19, question 74) |
| Arabic headword | The vowelled reading takes the word's place in the headword (كِتَاب), as Russian's stressed word does; a reading that differs beyond the vowel marks stands over the word (agent's recommendation, the owner gave no preference, 2026-10-19, question 75) |
| Persian ZWNJ | The zero-width non-joiner is ignored in the text and in headwords; a space after می/نمی and before ها/های/تر/ترین is also tried as no space (می خواهم → میخواهم) (agent's recommendation, the owner gave no preference, 2026-10-19, question 76) |
| Persian endings | Our own list of about 30 rules in the Persian bundle: plurals ها/های/ان, ezafe and indefinite ی/ای, possessives ام/ات/اش/مان/تان/شان, comparatives تر/ترین, verb prefixes می/نمی/ب/ن. 15,081 of the 20,000 commonest words against 11,014; a word that is itself a headword still comes first (owner, 2026-10-19, question 77) |
| Hebrew prefixes | Up to three of ו ה ב ל מ ש כ at a word's start are split off and the rest is looked up too (up to 17,570 of the 20,000 commonest words against 10,863); the whole word comes first. Checked so that words do not break: words that start with these letters still find themselves first, and the extra entries the split brings (מחשב → חשב) are counted on dictionary words (owner, 2026-10-19, question 78, "but it has to be checked that words don't break") |
| Scan length | 24 by default for Arabic, Persian and Hebrew; the setting stays per language. Longer than 24 are 0.2% of vowelled Arabic forms with a prefix, no Hebrew forms and 2.5% of Persian multiword verb forms, so nothing longer is needed (owner, 2026-10-19, question 79: "24, or even a bit more if objectively needed") |
| Fonts | The system's by default (Noto Naskh Arabic; Noto Sans and Serif Hebrew); downloadable: Arabic Noto Sans Arabic and Amiri, Persian Vazirmatn, Hebrew Heebo and Frank Ruhl Libre (OFL, from Google Fonts as the other catalog fonts); an Arabic-script font shows for Arabic and Persian where it has the letters (row "Font lists across languages") (owner, 2026-10-19, question 80) |
| Flags | Arabic Saudi Arabia, Persian Iran, Hebrew Israel; a default flag only (row "Flag country") (owner, 2026-10-19, question 81) |
| Bubble letters | ض (Arabic, "the language of dad"), پ (Persian, a letter Arabic lacks), א (Hebrew, the first letter) (owner, 2026-10-19, question 82) |
| Persian reading | The Latin reading follows the word in the headword line, normal size, grey (مجازی majâzi); it stays the Anki `{reading}` (owner, 2026-10-19, question 83) |
| Audio vowel marks | Recording titles that differ from the word only by vowel marks are found too. Exact titles from any source come first, then titles equal to the dictionary's vowelled reading, then the rest, which may be another word of the same letters (אב: father and the month Av) and plays only when nothing closer exists. Hebrew Wiktionary 1 → 5 of the twenty words, Arabic Lingua Libre 12 → 15 (owner, 2026-10-19, question 84) |
| Arabic dialect recordings | Not used for Arabic: Lingua Libre's dialect items (Levantine, Moroccan, Egyptian and others) are left out; dialects come with their own languages (Egyptian Arabic in group 5f) (owner, 2026-10-19, question 85) |

## Questions

69. Answered: (a). Urdu: with the three or apart. Urdu is printed in Nastaliq, unlike the Naskh of Arabic; ur-en has
    7.7k words; cloud recognition, Tesseract 1.4 MB, Paddle's Arabic model knows the letters but Nastaliq was not
    measured; no system Nastaliq font. (a, recommended) later, on its own, with a Nastaliq measurement; (b) as a fourth
    language now; (c) not at all.
70. Answered: other, "yes, but as a separate task after the research (and maybe the build) of group 5 and the build of
    group 4". Interface languages: (a, recommended) 14 left-to-right interface languages, right to left only for the
    studied text; (b) Arabic, Persian and Hebrew interfaces with mirrored screens.
71. Answered: (a). The popup: built left to right, no `dir` anywhere. (a, recommended) the layout stays, Arabic,
    Persian and Hebrew text runs right to left in place; (b) a mirrored popup for these languages.
72. Answered: (b). Arabic text in Anki fields: without a direction in the card template Anki shows an Arabic sentence
    left to right (punctuation and Latin runs misplaced). (a, recommended) plain text as Yomitan puts it, with a hint on
    `dir="auto"` in the template; (b) wrap Arabic and Hebrew fields in `<span dir="rtl">`.
73. Answered: (a), after the owner asked which is more correct and got a plainer explanation. Whether the wrap of
    question 72 covers the first field, which AnkiDroid's duplicate check compares with its markup: (a, recommended)
    every field but the first (one word shows right without a wrap, and notes added without it stay duplicates); (b)
    every field.
74. Answered: the agent's choice ("I don't know Arabic, do what is best"). Arabic spellings: dictionary headwords are
    written by the norm (أكبر, إسلام, على, لغة), live text often without hamza, with ي for ى and ه for ة. (a,
    recommended) broadly, as Greek's accents: without hamza matches with hamza anywhere, final ى/ي both ways, final ه
    finds ة, with hamza exactly (14,601 of 20,000 against 13,847; 292 shared spellings show both); (b) Yomitan's rules
    only.
75. Answered: no preference, so (a). The Arabic headword: (a, recommended) the vowelled reading in the word's place
    (كِتَاب), as Russian's stress; (b) small over the word, as Japanese readings; (c) no vowel marks.
76. Answered: no preference, so (a). The Persian ZWNJ, which recognition loses (joined or a space) and the
    dictionary's verb forms omit: (a, recommended) ignored, and a space after می/نمی and before ها/های/تر/ترین also
    tried as none; (b) ignored only, a space always separates.
77. Answered: (a). Persian endings: Yomitan has no Persian rules and the dictionary few noun forms. (a, recommended)
    our own list of about 30 rules (15,081 of 20,000 against 11,014); (b) the dictionary's forms only.
78. Answered: (a), "but it has to be checked that words don't break". Hebrew prefixes ו ה ב ל מ ש כ, up to three in a
    row (וכשהלכתי): (a, recommended) split off and the rest looked up too (up to 17,570 of 20,000 against 10,863; the
    whole word first, a letter that is no prefix brings an extra entry below: מחשב → חשב); (b) the dictionary only.
79. Answered: (a), "24, or even a bit more if objectively needed" (it is not). Scan length: each vowel mark counts as
    a character, so 15-18% of vowelled Arabic and Hebrew words with prefixes and 12% of Persian multiword verb forms
    exceed 16. (a, recommended) 24 for ar, fa, he; (b) 16 for all.
80. Answered: (a). Fonts: the system has Noto Naskh Arabic and Noto Sans and Serif Hebrew; the catalog nothing. (a,
    recommended) popular downloadable ones: Noto Sans Arabic, Amiri, Vazirmatn, Heebo, Frank Ruhl Libre; (b) the
    system's only.
81. Answered: (a). The Arabic flag (one flag per language; Persian Iran, Hebrew Israel): (a, recommended) Saudi
    Arabia; (b) Egypt; (c) the UAE.
82. Answered: (b). Bubble letters: (a, recommended) the first letter of the own name: ع ف ע; (b) typical letters: ض
    (Arabic is "the language of dad"), پ (Persian, not in Arabic), א (the first letter).
83. Answered: (a). The Persian reading, a Latin romanization for 97% of words: (a, recommended) after the word in the
    headword line, normal size, grey; (b) small over the word, as for languages without rules of their own; (c) not
    shown, kept for `{reading}`.
84. Answered: (a). Recording titles with vowel marks (He- 78%, Lingua Libre Arabic 17%), which exact matching misses:
    (a, recommended) exact first, then the dictionary's vowelled reading, then titles equal without marks
    (homographs last; Hebrew Wiktionary 1 → 5 of 20, Arabic Lingua Libre 12 → 15; 0.5-1.5 s per search); (b) exact and
    the vowelled reading only (Arabic Lingua Libre 14; Hebrew stays 1, its dictionary has no vowelled readings); (c)
    exact only, as now.
85. Answered: (a). Arabic dialect recordings on Lingua Libre (Levantine 4.3k, Moroccan 1.8k, Egyptian 950; 17 of the
    20 words, dialect pronunciation, some titles in Latin letters): (a, recommended) not used for Arabic; (b) a
    fallback after the Arabic recordings.
86. Answered: (a), after the owner said the span belongs in the field's template around our marker, added
    automatically or by a checkbox. How the span gets into the template
    (`<span dir="rtl">{cloze-prefix}<b>{cloze-body}</b>{cloze-suffix}</span>`; only markers of Arabic, Persian or
    Hebrew text; the first field without it): (a, recommended) automatically, by
    "Suggest templates" and the marker picker, removed by editing the field; (b) a "Right to left" checkbox under
    each field but the first, on by default for fields with the word or the sentence; (c) both.

## Work

Measured first on the emulator (shared: message the top session before using it), as they can change the work:

- QuickJS: load time, heap and per-call time of the Arabic bundle (7,157 regexes; 11.4 ms to load and up to 1.4 ms
  per call in Node) on vocalized and unvocalized lines.
- `local()` names of Noto Naskh Arabic, Noto Sans Hebrew and Noto Serif Hebrew; the WebView's fallback for Arabic
  without a font.
- Google's voices: `isLanguageAvailable` for ar, he, fa, and that `he` selects the Hebrew voice.
- Import of ar-en (992,134 form rows): time, size of the form-of table.
- Tesseract `heb` on pointed text (the comparison screens were unpointed).

Per language, on its branch:

- Catalog (`scripts/catalog/generate.mjs` `SOURCES`), the frequency lists, tessdata (`tessdata_catalog.py`), Paddle
  (`paddle_models.py`, arabic, listed once released), flags (`scripts/flags/`: sa, ir, il), fonts (catalog
  entries), the Yomitan bundle (`scripts/yomitan-language/build.mjs`, own processors and transforms), `Language`
  entry and its support class (word characters, sentence ends, quotes « » and the Hebrew ones, system fonts, audio
  order, scan length, bubble letter, flag), `OcrScript`, the popup reading rules (Arabic in place, Persian after the
  word), strings in all 14 locales (names of the rules included).
- Shared pieces on the Arabic branch (see the technical choices): popup `dir`, the span in suggested templates and
  picked markers (row "Anki fields"), app text in RTL views,
  Paddle order, cloud mixed lines, the glue, the audio fixes.
- Tests: unit tests of the support classes and every rule (rows of `notes/language-rules.md` with isolation tests);
  Yomitan's 382 Arabic cases through the glue; the research's test words with their expected forms; the Hebrew
  prefix check of row "Hebrew prefixes" on dictionary words; the Persian rules on the wordfreq list; `TextLayout`
  and `ReadingOrder` with RTL lines; `AccessibilityText` with an RTL text view; Paddle `readingOrder` with digits,
  brackets and a final stop; page tests of the popup's `dir`; the span in suggested templates and picked markers
  (none in the first field, none around meanings or Persian's reading); audio queries
  with marks and their order; `LanguageLookupTest` cases.
- Docs: `docs/usage.md`, `docs/architecture.md` (direction in the popup and in Anki); NOTICE (flags, wty, fonts,
  Paddle arabic once released).
- Test model: new cases after the group 4b and 4c ones; emulator checks in the run log. Test screens drawn in the
  emulator's browser or headless Chrome (Pillow cannot shape Arabic without raqm), with harakat, ZWNJ, both digit
  sets, brackets and mixed Latin.
