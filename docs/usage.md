# Using Screenlate

## First launch

1. Screenlate first asks which language you are learning, with Japanese preselected. The chosen language opens its download screen (see Languages). Updating from a version without this question keeps Japanese and its installed dictionaries and does not ask.
2. Open Screenlate and enable its accessibility service (the start screen links to the settings). Before the settings open, a dialog says what the service reads and where it goes; "Agree" opens them, and after that the button opens them at once. About → "What the service reads and sends" shows the same text. The chosen dictionaries download and install in the background; the start screen shows when they are ready.
3. If the switch in the accessibility settings is greyed out after installing from an APK file: App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings.
4. On devices with aggressive battery management, allow Screenlate to run in the background, or the system may stop the service. Settings → Background work shows whether battery optimization is on for Screenlate, asks the system to turn it off, and opens the manufacturer's startup settings on phones that have them.

## Navigation and camera

The bottom bar opens Home, Search, Camera and Settings. Switching tabs keeps each tab's open page and search text.
Tap the selected tab to return to its first page. Back from that page returns to Home; Back from Home leaves the app.
The bar is hidden during the first-launch setup and in the development image viewer. A page opened from outside the app
during the setup, such as Dictionaries from the selection-menu search, opens over it, and Back returns to the setup.

Camera opens the current photograph, or the rear camera when no photograph is stored. Allow camera access to take a
photo; a saved photograph can be viewed without this permission or the accessibility service. Tap the preview to focus,
pinch to zoom and tap the zoom label to return to 1×. The preview shows the whole frame the photograph gets, at its
size in the viewer; a blurred copy of the camera image fills the rest of the page (black with the e-ink theme). If the
camera has a flash, the upper flash button cycles through Off, Auto and On; it starts at Off when the app starts. Tap
the shutter to save the photograph privately in Screenlate.

The photograph fits the available area and survives tab changes, backgrounding and restarting the device. New photo
opens the camera; Close or Back returns to the previous photograph. A successful capture replaces it; a failed capture
keeps it. Leaving capture releases the camera. Camera errors offer an explanation or retry. The ✕ at the left of the
top bar closes the photograph and opens the camera. A photograph in the history stays there; one that is not asks
first whether to save it to the history or delete it.

Recognition starts after a successful capture and continues when you switch tabs or put the app in the background.
The photograph is read as a whole, scaled down to 1500 pixels on its longer side. With High accuracy on in the Camera
settings, a large photograph is read in overlapping parts at full resolution instead, and once more as a whole for
lettering too large to fit in one part: small text is recognized better, but several times slower, and cloud
recognition uses more mobile data. Recognize more accurately, first in the ⋮ menu, reads the open photograph in parts
once; it is shown for a photograph larger than one part that was read as a whole. With both engines, a photograph is
read by cloud recognition, and on the device only when cloud recognition fails.
Camera remembers its own language, initially the active language, and offers only enabled languages. While its
language is turned off, Camera uses the active language and returns to its own when it is turned on again. The language
chip above the photograph, and over the preview, lists them with their flags, marks the active language and the
languages that lack a dictionary or a recognition model, and links to the language settings. The shared Cloud, Device
and Both settings and that language's local model apply. The strip under the top bar shows the recognition status:
the recognized regions and the time, or the progress of a running recognition. Changing the language or recognition
settings highlights the strip and names the change: tap Recognize to apply it. Turning High accuracy on counts as such
a change for a photograph read as a whole; turning it off leaves photographs read in parts as they are. Existing text keeps its actual
recognition language until the new attempt finishes. A warning next to Recognize explains incomplete recognition,
marks the missing regions on a small picture of the photograph and retries only those regions.
After an app restart, interrupted work waits for this explicit retry; it never resumes network requests on its own.

Pan with one finger and pinch to zoom up to 8× the fitted size. Returning to the photograph fits it again. Pull the
bubble over it to look up stored text; the glyph and dictionary follow the photo's recognition language. A tap briefly
highlights recognized text. Moving, zooming and bubble gestures reuse saved coordinates without recognizing again;
the popup follows its word as the photograph moves.
The bubble reads only the photograph while this viewer is open; bars and empty margins are outside it. It never scans
the live preview, dialogs or an unrecognized photograph. Leaving the viewer docks the bubble (while you drag it, when
you lift the finger) and other pages retain ordinary screen lookup. The accessibility service is needed for the bubble,
independently of capture and viewing.

To attach part of a photograph to Anki, hold the popup's add button. The crop editor fits the original photo above
its controls and starts around the word's paragraph. Move the frame or its corners, switch between Frame and Whole
photo, then tap Add. The attachment uses the original pixels, independently of the viewer's zoom, scaled down to about
4 megapixels when larger (the size of a phone screenshot). Cancel, or leaving
the photo while the editor is open, closes it without adding the note. Notes, sentence context, audio and translations use the language
of the recognized text.

Save to gallery in the ⋮ menu exports an ordinary original photograph to Pictures/Screenlate, preserving
its quality and orientation. It requires no broad storage permission. The copy contains no saved OCR coordinates;
replacing or deleting the private photograph does not delete the exported copy. Nothing is exported automatically.

### Images from the phone

The round button left of the shutter, Import from gallery in the ⋮ menu, and the button under the camera permission
request open the system photo picker (the file picker on phones without one); it gives the app access to the chosen
image alone. "Share → Screenlate" in another app does the same with one image and opens the Camera tab (during the
first-launch setup, the image waits until the Camera tab is opened). The image is
copied into the app and becomes the open photograph, as a new capture does: it is recognized right away in the Camera
tab's language, and with Save automatically on it is added to the history. When the open photograph is not in the
history (saving is manual, or saving it automatically failed), the app first asks whether to save it or delete it;
Cancel keeps it and drops the image.
Closing the picker changes nothing. JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIF images are kept as they are; other formats (GIF, BMP and
the like) are stored as PNG, scaled down beyond 24 megapixels. Files larger than 100 MB are not imported; a message
says so. The history shows the time of the import, not when the image was made.

### Photo history

The history keeps photographs privately in Screenlate with their recognized text, apart from the gallery. Save to
history in the ⋮ menu adds the open photograph while it is not in the history; with Save automatically on, every new
photograph, taken or imported, is added right away, and turning the switch on adds the open one. A photograph replaced
by a new capture or an import stays in the history; one that is not in it is deleted.

The round button right of the shutter shows the latest photograph in the history and opens the history page (the ⋮
menu has History as well). The page lists the photographs newest first, each with its date and the flag of its
recognition language. Tap one to open it on the Camera tab with its saved text; it is not recognized again. When the
open photograph is not in the history, the app first asks whether to save it or delete it.
Long-press to select photographs, tap to add or remove more, and delete them with the trash icon.

Camera settings (in the ⋮ menu, and in Settings before Appearance) start with Recognition: the High accuracy switch,
and Engines, which shows the engines photographs share with screen recognition and opens them on the Text recognition page.
Below, they hold Save automatically, the photo limit and the size limit in MB, the history's photo count and size, and Clear history. A limit of 0 means none; beyond a limit the
oldest photographs are deleted, while the latest one always stays. Lowering a limit below the current history asks
first and names how many photographs will be deleted. A photograph deleted from the history, by the page, a limit or
Clear history, is deleted completely: when it is open on the Camera tab, the camera opens instead. An open photograph
that was never saved to the history stays.

## Languages

- The active language is the one scans, search and the popup use. Each language keeps its own dictionaries and its own lookup, popup, Anki and audio settings; the other settings are shared.
- The Languages card on the start screen shows the quick languages as round flags, the active one ringed, with the active language's name and installation status; a tap on another flag makes it active. Quick languages are the active one plus up to two starred on the Languages screen. "All languages" opens that screen. With e-ink mode on, flags are replaced by two-letter language codes.
- The Languages screen lists every language Screenlate supports:
  - **Turned on**: the radio button picks the active language; the star adds a language to quick switching (the active one always has it). Switching to a starred language swaps the two: the previous active one stays starred. Switching to a language without a star (or turning one on) moves the active star to it, and the previous active language loses its star. A language spoken in several countries has a "Flag" button that picks the country of its flag (English: United States by default). "Turn off" is there while more than one language is on. Chinese also has "Characters", Simplified or Traditional: a word is shown in the glyph forms of its own script, else of the text around it, else of this choice; words found by pinyin are shown in it, and Wiktionary's Traditional entries found through a Simplified spelling show the spelling that was read. "All words in these characters" (off by default) also looks up the text converted into the chosen script and shows each word in it where a dictionary has that spelling. The default is Traditional when the interface language is Traditional Chinese, else Simplified. A backup keeps the choice with the turned-on languages; only the full settings reset returns it to the default.
  - **Turned off**: languages that left dictionaries or recognition models, with their number and size. A tap lists them with their sizes; a dictionary can be deleted alone or have its languages changed. "Delete all" deletes the language's files; "Turn on" turns it on. Languages of dictionaries this version does not support are listed here too, without "Turn on".
  - **Not turned on**: the other languages. "Turn on" turns a language on at once when its mandatory dictionaries are still installed, and opens its download screen otherwise.
- The download screen. "Languages of definitions" lists the languages the dictionaries explain words in: the interface language and English. When the interface language is English or the learned language, or has no dictionaries, a button picks another one (or none). Below, a card per category (Main dictionary, Word forms, Frequency, Pronunciation, Short translations, Characters, On-device recognition) lists the dictionaries with their size and license. On-device recognition lists the language's downloadable models (Tesseract, PaddleOCR) with a "Main model" switch on the ticked ones; a model that needs a text detector downloads it with itself, and its size includes it. For a language the built-in recognizer (ML Kit) reads, it comes first, built in and needing no download, and is the main model; the other models are unticked, the more accurate ones marked so, and a model ticked there becomes the main one unless the switch stays on ML Kit. For any other language the recommended model is ticked (the Tesseract models of Thai and Khmer say that they often misread busy screens); a language it does not read and without a model is recognized in the cloud only: scans need network, and with Engines set to device only a scan says the device cannot read it. Recommended ones are ticked; the only dictionary of a mandatory category is always taken; installed ones say so. ⓘ next to Pronunciation shows what such a dictionary gives. The frequency dictionary is built on the device from a word list that is downloaded instead of an archive; once imported it sorts the language's results, unless another frequency dictionary was chosen for that. The button at the bottom shows the download size, and the line under it about how much space the dictionaries take after import. It stays disabled while a mandatory category has nothing ticked or the free space is short (the space after import plus the largest archive). On mobile data, a download over 50 MB asks first.
- Confirming turns the language on and makes it active at once. Selected dictionaries already installed are enabled without downloading them again. The home screen shows the active language's readiness, on-device recognition availability, remaining installations and failures. "Installation status" opens the Dictionaries screen with the full queue, including recognition models.
- Until the mandatory dictionaries and required recognition components are ready, scanning shows an explanation and a link to installation status before reading text or capturing the screen. Typed search also shows the status while its mandatory dictionaries are missing; it searches automatically once they become ready. Optional downloads remain visible on Home after lookup is available. When on-device recognition is unavailable, cloud recognition can be used if enabled; "App text only" needs no recognition model.
- Turning a language off keeps its settings; they come back when it is turned on again. If the active language is turned off, the first starred one becomes active, otherwise the first one left. "Delete its dictionaries and models" is ticked by default and also cancels the language's unfinished downloads and bundled installation, even before the first file is installed (the size is then zero); untick it to keep the files and downloads. Kept files appear under "Turned off" on the Languages screen.
- Croatian and Serbian are separate languages that read the same Serbo-Croatian dictionaries, downloaded once. Each has its own on-device model, voice, flag and lookup, popup, Anki and audio settings, and is turned on and switched to by itself. Serbian reads Cyrillic and Latin text: lookup, the voice, sentence translation and cloud recognition take either script; its on-device model is Cyrillic by default, and a Latin one can be chosen under Text recognition → On-device model. Croatian pronunciation audio tries Croatian recordings first, then Serbian ones of the same word. Turning one off keeps the dictionaries while the other is on. With both off, "Turned off" lists their files once, with a "Turn on" button for each language.
- Slovak also reads the Czech dictionaries, as a fallback for the common words its own dictionaries lack. Its download screen lists Czech's main dictionaries after Slovak's own, unticked and marked "Fallback dictionary (Czech)"; they do not count for the mandatory category. Installed Czech dictionaries show in Slovak's list on the Dictionaries screen, and they stay while Slovak is on, even with Czech turned off.

## The bubble

| Gesture | Result |
|---|---|
| Pull the bubble away from the screen edge | The screen is scanned; aim at a word to see its entry. |
| Move the floating bubble | The entry follows the aim point. The screen is not scanned again. |
| Tap the floating bubble | Scan again, e.g. after scrolling. Recognized lines flash briefly. |
| Hold the floating bubble | A menu: copy the paragraph under the aim (a whole speech bubble or paragraph, without line breaks) or all recognized text, or open Screenlate (the bubble docks; not shown while Screenlate is in front, as over a photograph). With nothing to copy, no language row and Screenlate in front, holding the bubble opens no menu. With more than one quick language, a row of their flags above (codes in e-ink mode; TalkBack reads the language names) switches the active language: the docked bubble's glyph changes at once, and the next scan (a tap on the bubble or pulling it out) uses the new language; the open scan keeps its language. With the aim off text, the paragraph of the word shown in the popup is copied. The menu opens above the bubble, or below it when there is no room above. Back, a tap anywhere outside or turning the screen off closes it; that tap does nothing else. |
| Double tap the floating bubble | Switch the aim point between "above the finger" and "bubble center". |
| Press ✕ | Close the entry; the bubble stays where it is and keeps the recognized text. The entry stays closed until the bubble moves or scans again. |
| Drag the bubble to the left or right edge | Close the entry and dock the bubble: once the bubble's center is past the edge, or when the finger is lifted at the edge. With "Top and bottom edges" on, the top and bottom edges dock it too. |
| Drag the docked bubble along the edge | Move the dock. |
| Hold the docked bubble | The same menu without the copy items: the language flags and Open Screenlate. A move before the hold pulls the bubble out or moves the dock instead. |

A word broken at the end of a line or column is looked up as a whole, reading on in the next line or column.

When no word is found at the aim, no entry opens; if the character there is a kanji that a kanji dictionary describes, its kanji entry opens instead. The Search screen does the same for its text.

The entry opens above the word when it fits there, otherwise below the bubble, and covers neither; vertical text is treated the same. When neither has room for all of it, it keeps its size at the edge of the screen on the roomier side and covers part of the word or the bubble rather than getting low or narrow. In landscape it opens beside the word, on the side with more room, nearly as tall as the screen and a little narrower when it has to; when no side has that much room, it covers part of the word the same way.

The Quick Settings tile, the start screen and the "Show the bubble" switch in the bubble settings hide and show the bubble; holding the tile opens Screenlate. Turning the screen closes the entry and docks the bubble on the same side.

The docked bubble shows the active language's letter: the first letter of the language's name in that language (D for Deutsch, Р for русский), or a typical character of a script of its own (あ, 한). When two quick languages have letters that look alike (English and Spanish, Russian and Polish), both show their two-letter codes instead (EN, ES); Cyrillic-script languages write their codes in Cyrillic (РУ), also in e-ink mode.

A docked bubble stays clear of the status and navigation bars and of the strips along the edges where a swipe belongs to the system (the notifications at the top, going home at the bottom), so pulling it out is never taken for a system gesture; an open keyboard lifts a bottom dock above it. Where nothing takes a touch at the screen's edge, such as the left and right edges, it sits at the very edge. In fullscreen apps, where the bars are hidden, it goes to the very edge at the top and bottom too and shows more of itself, so that a third of it stands past the system's strip: pull it out by that part. Where the strip is wider than that allows, it stays where it is with the bars shown.

## Settings

The start screen keeps the search, the service status and warnings; everything else is under Settings:

- **Bubble**: whether the bubble shows, aim point, dock side, "Top and bottom edges" (off by default: the bubble docks only at the left and right), bubble size, word highlight, vibration, and "Hide the bubble in apps", which says in how many apps the bubble hides and opens their list: the launcher apps with a search, the hidden ones first; the bubble disappears while a checked app is open.
- **Text recognition**: how the bubble reads the screen and the camera reads photographs.
  - **Screen recognition** (the default text source): a screenshot is recognized in the cloud; an on-device draft of the lines around the aim appears first. When cloud recognition fails (no network, no answer within 15 seconds, an error), the on-device result stays and ⚠ appears next to the text source; tap it for the reason. Cloud recognition uses mobile data, about 0.1–0.4 MB per scan. Touching the bubble opens the connection to it in advance, with a request that carries no screen content, so that the first scan after a pause is not slower.
  - **Engines**: both (the default), cloud only or device only. With both, on-device recognition reads the text around the aim first, so words are available almost immediately; the more accurate cloud result replaces it when it arrives. Without a network connection or on a cloud error, the on-device result is kept. Turning one engine off is intended for low-end or heavily loaded devices: with cloud only, nothing is recognized without a network connection and words are available only after the server responds; device only works offline but recognizes vertical text and manga less accurately, OCR boost is unavailable, and the text source shows "On-device".
  - **OCR boost** sends parts of the screen to cloud recognition separately, so that small text missed by a full-screen scan is recognized: on demand (the part under the aim, when nothing was found there) or always. Turn it on only if small text is not recognized: each part is an extra request and uses more mobile data.
  - **Reduce load on the device** (off by default): the device reads the lines around the aim at once, but the whole screen only when cloud recognition has not responded within 3 seconds. Reduces load and battery use; words away from the aim become available later.
  - **On-device model**: each turned-on language with the model it reads with; a tap opens its list: the built-in recognizer (ML Kit) where it reads the script, and the catalog models with their size, the recommended one marked, ⓘ for what each is good at. The Tesseract models of Thai and Khmer say that they often misread text on busy screens and that cloud recognition is more accurate. A model is downloaded from there and used once chosen; a language without a choice uses the built-in recognizer where it reads the script, else its recommended model once installed, else the best installed one. A model whose files turn out to be damaged is not used: the start screen lists it with "Download again" and "Remove". A model in use can be deleted only after another one is chosen; a model other languages also have lists them when it is deleted.
  - **App text → Read app text** (experimental): the app's own text is used where the app exposes character positions. It is exact, available immediately and works offline. The screen is still recognized alongside for everything else, such as text in images; whichever is ready first is shown. Some apps report incorrect character positions, which shifts the highlight or selects a neighboring word; in that case, turn the option off.
  - **App text → App text only**: no screenshots and no recognition, only the text apps expose. Text in images, manga and games is not read, and apps that do not expose their text show a message instead. Intended for e-ink readers, older or low-end devices where recognition is unavailable or slow, and slow or metered connections. The screen recognition settings above are off while it is on; a picture for an Anki note is still taken when asked for.
  - **Test → OCR test**: recognizes an image picked on the phone as a scan does and shows each engine's text and time.
- **Lookup**: how many characters from the aim point are considered (16 by default, 24 in Georgian, Armenian, Arabic, Persian and Hebrew, whose words run long, and in Swedish, Danish and Norwegian, which write compounds as one word), how many entries a lookup shows (32; larger values are slower), romaji typed or recognized as Latin text looked up as kana (taberu → たべる), and the kanji of the matched word shown as their own entries below the results. Without romaji, Latin text is looked up when Japanese follows it (Tシャツ) or when the whole word is a dictionary entry (OL, DNA, CD-ROM), wherever the aim is inside it; single letters are skipped, and letter case counts.
- **Dictionaries**, **Anki**, **Audio**, **Popup**, **Translation**, **Appearance**: see below.
- **Background work**: battery optimization for Screenlate and the manufacturer's startup settings, the two things that can stop the bubble in the background. "Show a notification while the service runs" (under Persistent notification, off by default) keeps a minimum-priority notification; systems that stop background apps stop apps with such a notification much less often. While notifications are off for Screenlate, the service still runs but its notification is not shown; the card says so and offers to allow them. When the service stops (turned off in the settings or by the system) and has not come back a few seconds later, a notification says the bubble is gone; a tap on it opens the accessibility settings, and it goes away once the service runs again. It needs notifications to be allowed. "If the service turns off" explains what to do when the system keeps turning the accessibility service off: the phone's security app may restrict apps installed outside an app store; add Screenlate to its list of allowed or trusted apps and turn the service on again.
- **Backup and restore**: backups and the import from Yomitan; see below.

The ⟲ icon at the top of the Settings list resets every setting to its default: the bubble (whether it shows, its place, size and hidden apps), text recognition, lookup, Anki, audio, the popup, sentence translation, the characters chosen for Chinese, the appearance, background work and the update announcements; hints, one-time offers and the accessibility service dialog are shown again. The dictionaries, turned-on languages, installed fonts and the interface language are kept. Bubble, Text recognition, Lookup, Anki, Audio, Popup, Translation, Appearance and Background work each have the same icon for their own settings; a page reset affects only that page, and the battery and startup settings, which belong to Android, are never changed. Every reset asks first. Holding an icon button shows what it does.

When something breaks outside Screenlate (AnkiDroid removed or its permission revoked, the note type or deck deleted, fields renamed, the accessibility service turned off, dictionary files missing, recognition model files damaged, audio sources failing), the start screen says what and links to the fix; several missing dictionaries share one card, as do damaged models, and so do a language's failed audio sources. A failed audio source is listed under the language of the lookup it failed in; a source several languages use is listed under each language whose lookups it failed in. With several languages turned on, a note setup or audio problem names its language, and its button opens that language's settings. In the popup, ➕ turns grey; tapping it shows the reason and a button that opens the Anki settings of the popup's language (over a photo, the photo's recognition language).

## Entries

- Tap a link inside a definition to look it up; the back arrow returns.
- Tap a kanji in the headword to see its kanji dictionary entry (KANJIDIC for Japanese, CC-CEDICT Hanzi for Chinese and, for Korean, a hanja dictionary built from the Unicode Han Database that comes with the app; each is ticked on its language's download screen). The entry shows the lists the character is on, such as jouyou or the basic hanja for education.
- Next to the word: how the text was inflected (🧩, the first chain of rules when several lead to the word), one frequency, the most frequent value of the first frequency dictionary (the sorting one when chosen), and up to two pitch accents or transcriptions (from pronunciation dictionaries); "+N" shows the N hidden chains, values, accents or transcriptions, "−" hides them again. A dictionary may give several values, such as one for the word written in kana (marked ㋕). A frequency dictionary's name is shown whole when it is up to four letters long (a CJK character counts as two), else as its first letter (BCCWJ → B); a tap on the chip shows the whole name, another tap shortens it again, and "+N" shows the first dictionary's name whole. When the text is the headword itself, no chain stands beside it, though the dictionary may also list the word as a form of itself (Croatian kuća, also its genitive plural; Hebrew שלום, whose pointed construct form שְׁלוֹם has the same letters); text written as the form (שְׁלוֹם) shows the form's name.
- Transcriptions carry the regions their dictionary names (US, UK, AU…) and follow the order of the Regions list of the audio sources (see Audio sources below); those without a region come last. In a language without such a list they keep the dictionary's order. A long list opens five at a time: "+N" shows the first five, "Show all" the rest. Transcriptions in ASCII notations (SAMPA, X-SAMPA) are left out, and a phonetic form in […] that matches a phonemic one in /…/ is shown once. Regions are read when a dictionary is imported; import an older one again to get them.
- Tap an inflection step 🧩, a pitch accent, a transcription or a tag with a description (dotted underline, or a label such as "noun" in dictionaries that describe their labels) to read the explanation at the bottom of the popup. Tag descriptions come from the dictionary's tag list and are kept for dictionaries imported from this version on; import an older dictionary again to get them.
- 🔊 plays the pronunciation from the configured audio sources; hold it to choose among all recordings found.
- ➕ adds a note to Anki; hold it to attach a picture: a crop editor opens with the word's paragraph and some space around it selected. "Whole screen" selects the whole screenshot and turns into "Frame", which brings the previous frame back.
- The note takes the sentence from the text as it is shown when ➕ is pressed. In languages written with spaces, a single capital letter with a period before a capitalized word is taken as an initial (J. Smith) and ends no sentence. Quotes that recognition reads as plain characters are written as the language's: >> and << at the edge of a word as » and « in a language that quotes with them; a " right after a full stop, ! or ?, or at the end of a word, as the closing quote of the latest quote still open („Vino aici!” in Romanian, „Jes.“ in Esperanto); and a " at the start of a word, with no quote open, as the language's opening quote (“Karibu!” in Swahili); text an app gives is taken as written. While the scan still refines its text (the spinner turns), ➕ is grey and a tap only says to wait, so no note gets its sentence from the draft; a word read from the app's own text can be added at once. A new scan closes the popup of the previous one. Docking the bubble does not stop a note already being added; an open crop editor closes as cancelled.
- Hold text in an entry to select it, drag the handles to widen the selection, and tap Copy above it; furigana are left out. Back or a tap outside the popup ends the selection.
- The copy button in an entry's header copies the word. With definition copying on (Popup settings), a copy button next to each dictionary's name copies that dictionary's definitions for the entry, without the word.

Text selected in any app can also be looked up through the "Look up in Screenlate" entry of the selection menu, and the Search screen accepts typed text.

In Chinese, the Search screen also finds words by pinyin typed in Latin letters, with or without tones: "nihao", "ni hao", "ni3hao3" and "nǐhǎo" all find 你好, and ü can be typed as v. Typed tones and an apostrophe between syllables (xi'an) rank the words that have them first; otherwise the frequency dictionary decides. Words are shown in the script chosen on the Languages screen. A dictionary imported by an earlier version must be imported again for this.

In English, a phrasal verb in the past, -ing or third-person form ("picked up", "gives up") finds its phrase even when the dictionary gives the phrase no part of speech, as Wiktionary dictionaries often do, when the word after the verb is an adverb particle (up, out, off, back and the like). After a preposition (to, for, with) the phrase is found only when the dictionary marks it as a verb, so "closed to traffic" does not give "close to" (near). A phrase with an object in between ("picked the phone up") is found only when the dictionary marks it as a verb.

In German, Dutch, Swedish, Norwegian and Danish, a compound without an entry of its own is shown as its parts in order, under a line that names them ("Compound: Küchentisch + Lampenschirm"): the split into the fewest dictionary words, and of those the one whose shortest part is longest (Küchentischlampenschirm → Küchentisch, Lampenschirm; Dutch stadsbestuurder → stads, bestuurder, not stadsbestuur, der). A linking element between two parts is skipped: German -s-, -es- or -n- (Arbeitsschreibtisch → Arbeit, Schreibtisch), Dutch -s-, -en- or -e-, Swedish -s-, -e-, -u-, -o- or -a- (statsminister → stat, minister; skattepengar → skatt, pengar), Norwegian and Danish -s- or -e- (arbeidsplass → arbeid, plass; barnehage → barn, hage; Danish skolebørn → skole, børn). A Swedish part shortened before its link (kyrko- in kyrkogård, of kyrka) is not found. Parts are at least three letters long; a compound with its own entry is shown as it is. ## Popup

- **Popup elements.** "Show the recognized text above the entries" (on by default): the popup starts with the recognized text around the aim and the recognition source; off, it starts right with the entry. The translation button (see Sentence translation below), pitch accent colors or tone colors and definition copying, described below, are set in the same card; with several languages turned on, pitch accent colors are in the Japanese card and tone colors in the Chinese one.
- **Auto-hide.** Both off by default. "After adding to Anki": the popup closes once ➕ has added or updated the note, if it still shows that word. "When the aim leaves the word": while the bubble moves, the popup closes once the aim is about 3 mm away from the word, on no text (or text the current text source does not read); moving onto another word shows that word as usual. Messages (no text found, errors) stay. Only the popup closes: the bubble stays where it is and keeps the recognized text.
- **Popup font.** By default the popup and the search page use the phone's own font for the language: its Japanese font for Japanese, so kanji keep their Japanese forms (compare 直 or 骨) even where the phone's main font is Chinese, its Chinese font for Chinese, with the Simplified or Traditional forms of each word's script, and its Korean font for Korean, so hanja keep their Korean forms. Free fonts for the language can be downloaded from the list (Japanese: Noto Sans JP, Noto Serif JP, BIZ UDPGothic, BIZ UDPMincho, Klee One, Zen Maru Gothic, M PLUS 1p; Chinese: Noto Sans SC and Noto Serif SC with Simplified forms, Noto Sans TC, Noto Serif TC and LXGW WenKai TC with Traditional ones; Korean: Noto Sans KR, Noto Serif KR, Nanum Gothic, Nanum Myeongjo; Hindi: Mukta, Hind, Tiro Devanagari Hindi; Bengali: Hind Siliguri, Tiro Bangla; Thai: Sarabun, Noto Sans Thai Looped, Kanit, Prompt; Khmer: Kantumruy Pro, Hanuman; Arabic and Persian: Noto Sans Arabic, Amiri, Vazirmatn; Hebrew: Heebo, Frank Ruhl Libre; languages written in Latin letters: Literata, Inter, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Lexend, PT Serif, PT Sans), or a font file (`.ttf`, `.otf`, `.ttc`) added. A language's list shows the fonts downloaded for it and every added file; a Latin font is listed, and once downloaded shown, for every language whose letters it has (Literata and Inter also cover Cyrillic, Greek and Vietnamese, Lexend Vietnamese, the PT fonts Cyrillic; these four have every Kazakh and Kyrgyz letter), and an Arabic-script font for Arabic and Persian. A Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Hindi, Bengali, Thai, Khmer or Hebrew font, or an Arabic-script one, shows on another language's list only while it is the chosen one there. Deleting a font asks first; languages that used it go back to the phone's font. Each added file is a font of its own, named by its family and style ("Noto Serif JP Bold"); a file with the same name replaces the earlier one. Web fonts (`.woff`, `.woff2`) cannot be added: convert them to TTF or OTF first. A downloaded or added font is used only for Japanese text by default, so Latin and Cyrillic letters keep the phone's font; turn off "Only for Japanese text" (or the language's switch) to use it for everything.
- **Text size, weight and letter thickness** apply to every popup font. The weight (normal, medium, semi-bold, bold) uses the weights available in the font; a font with a single weight switches from normal directly to bold. Letter thickness draws a thin outline around each letter, which works with any font and adds to the weight. Both are off by default and follow the "Only for Japanese text" switch: with it on, or with the phone's font, they apply only to Japanese characters; with it off, to all text. The e-ink mode's "Make larger" changes only sizes.
- **Custom CSS.** Applied to the popup and the search page after the dictionaries' own styles, like Yomitan's custom popup CSS. Entries use Yomitan's class names, and badges follow Yomitan's tag color variables such as `--tag-frequency-background-color`. Warnings above the field point to lines the browser would skip and to fonts the phone does not have; such fonts fall back to the popup font. Fonts the CSS defines itself with `@font-face` are left as they are. A warning also names each server the CSS loads files from: stylesheets and fonts from the internet load (that server then sees when you look up words), other files such as images are blocked. Names of common Windows and macOS fonts stand for the phone's sans-serif or serif font of their language: Japanese (Meiryo, Yu Gothic, Yu Mincho, MS Mincho, Hiragino), Chinese (Microsoft YaHei, SimHei, SimSun, PingFang, MingLiU) and Korean (Malgun Gothic, Gulim, Dotum, Batang, Apple SD Gothic Neo).
- **Pitch accent colors** (Japanese, off by default; needs a pitch accent dictionary). The word and its reading take the color of the first pitch accent the dictionaries give, in their order, and each accent next to the word takes the color of its own type: heiban (no downstep) blue, atamadaka (downstep after the first mora) red, nakadaka (inside the word) orange, odaka (after the last mora) green, kifuku (a downstep in a verb or i-adjective) purple. Custom CSS can change them through `--pitch-heiban-color`, `--pitch-atamadaka-color`, `--pitch-nakadaka-color`, `--pitch-odaka-color` and `--pitch-kifuku-color`.
- **Tone colors** (Chinese, off by default). Chinese headwords show their pinyin one syllable above each character, and with this switch each syllable takes the color of its tone: first red, second orange, third green, fourth blue, neutral grey; the readings in a character's entry too. Custom CSS can change them through `--tone-1-color` to `--tone-5-color` (5 is the neutral tone).
- **Definition copying** (off by default) adds a copy button next to each dictionary's name in the popup and the search page. "Everything" copies all the dictionary shows for the entry: apps that paste formatted text get it formatted, others get plain text. "Only meanings" copies the numbered meanings as plain text, leaving out examples, notes, cross-references and readings; it works with dictionaries that mark their meanings (JMdict-based dictionaries, many Japanese monolingual dictionaries) and with numbered or line-per-meaning text. It is not guaranteed for every dictionary: when the meanings cannot be told apart, the whole text is copied.
- Importing a Yomitan settings export can bring over the text size and the custom popup CSS.

## Sentence translation

A free online service translates the sentence around the word, without an account or key. The translation is machine-made and may be inaccurate, and the sentence is sent to the service's server. Settings → Translation holds:

- **Translation button** (off by default; an update from version 0.2.3 or earlier, or a restored backup of such a version, keeps it on unless it was turned off). In the popup, the button sits in the header before the text source; with the recognized text hidden, it is among the first entry's buttons. A tap shows the sentence's translation under the header with the name of the service that made it and a copy button, which copies the sentence and the translation on two lines; a second tap hides it. A new word, or a popup opened again, starts without the translation; a refined scan result for the same word and sentence keeps it. The same switch is under Popup → Popup elements.
- **Translation in Anki notes** (on by default). The `{sentence-translation}` marker gets the translation the popup shows; without one, ➕ asks for it right away and the note waits for it up to 5 seconds (counted after the crop editor when ➕ is held); the field stays empty if none comes. The same switch is in the Anki settings above the deck. Pre-filled templates put the marker into a field named for a sentence translation (SentenceTranslation, Sentence Meaning, SentenceEnglish and the like; a field named only Translation stays empty); otherwise add it with the + button of a field.
- **Services**: Bing, Google and Edge, all on, in that order. The first enabled service translates the sentence; if it returns an error or does not respond within 3 seconds, the next one is used. Drag a row's handle to change the order. Bing is usually the most accurate; its first request in an hour takes about a second longer. When every service fails, the popup lists each one's reason (no connection, timeout, the service limits requests, a secure connection that failed, a certificate that is not valid for the device's date and time, and so on).
- **Translate into**: the interface language by default (English when the interface is Japanese). The list can be searched by the language's name in the interface language, in the language itself or in English, and languages can be starred to keep them at the top. Some languages are translated by only some services; the list says so.
- **Test**: asks every service that is turned on at once and shows each one's answer and how long it took.

A translation is kept for 10 minutes, so adding a note after reading the translation sends no new request.

## Appearance

- **Theme**: the system's, light or dark.
- **Colors** (Android 12 and later): Screenlate's own violet colors with a pink accent on switches, sliders, checkboxes, progress and the main buttons (the default), or System: the colors the phone picks for apps, usually from the wallpaper.
- **Language** of the interface: the system's, or English, Russian, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Polish, Turkish, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified or Traditional). It can also be set in the system settings: Apps → Screenlate → Language. Translations were made with AI and have not been reviewed by native speakers; a note under the picker says so.

Turning it on offers a larger bubble and larger popup text; turning it off, with the switch or by restoring a backup, brings back the previous sizes, except those changed by hand in the meantime (sizes stored in the backup take precedence). On devices that look like e-ink readers the start screen offers the mode once, with a link to "App text only".

Translations into English, into the language itself and into the interface language come first; the other translation languages are gathered under "Other languages" with the number of languages, each of them a part of its own inside; the other kinds follow under "Supplementary dictionaries". At first only the translations into the interface language are open, and any part with a download under way or an update waiting (with "Other languages" around it); a part opened or closed by hand stays so while Screenlate runs.
- Import Yomitan archives (`.zip`) from a file, download dictionaries from the catalog, or import a Yomitan "dictionary collection" export (`.json`) to bring over everything installed in Yomitan at once; its button opens the Yomitan card of the backup screen.
- "Check for updates" asks each dictionary's index whether a newer revision exists, and lists recognition models whose catalog entry names another version, each with the turned-on languages that read with it; an installed model keeps working until its update replaces it.
- An old build of a catalog dictionary that cannot update itself (imported from elsewhere, without an update address) shows an update icon on its catalog card and is listed by "Check for updates"; updating puts the current build in its place, keeping its position and switch.
- Each dictionary shows its languages (words → explanations). They come from the dictionary's own description, else from the catalog, else from its content: the script of the headwords and definitions, with Latin text identified by the phone's on-device language detection. Dictionaries installed before this was added get theirs once, from sample lookups. Frequency and pitch dictionaries show only the language of their words. Tap a dictionary and "Change" to set them by hand; a dictionary is used only for lookups in its word language.
- Imports and downloads show their progress in a notification. On Android 13 and later the first import or download asks for the notification permission; while it is missing, the Dictionaries screen offers it again, and once Android stops asking, its button opens the app's notification settings. Before each request a short dialog says what the permission is for: the progress of imports and downloads, and keeping the bubble's service running (the switch under Background work); OK, Back or a tap outside go on to the request.
- "Cancel" on a running or waiting import or download, or in its notification, cancels only that one; the others go on. A download stops at once; a dictionary the engine is already importing is finished first and then dropped. The notification hides "Cancel" during the last step, while the dictionary is being added to the list.
- Downloads wait for a network connection; while there is none, a waiting download's card and its catalog card say "Waiting for a network", and the card of several waiting tasks says so under its title. Two tasks run at a time; their imports go one after another, so a downloaded dictionary or model may wait for the other one's import. Starting a download that is already queued or running (from the catalog, the language setup, the start screen or "Check for updates") does not queue it again; when its earlier download stopped and its card is still there, the new one continues it.
- A download whose network goes away waits for it: its card says "Waiting for a network", and it continues from where it stopped once a network is back. This does not count as a failed attempt, also when it happens during the last one.
- A download that breaks off while the network stays up (no data for 20 seconds, server error) goes to the end of the queue and retries by itself up to three times, after 30 seconds, one minute and two minutes, continuing from where it stopped when the server supports it; its card shows the attempt, and the tasks behind it go on meanwhile. After that, or after any other failure, the card offers "Resume" and "Cancel"; "Cancel" deletes the partial file. A download that stopped, failed or was cancelled stays so until "Resume" or a new download of it.
- Two or more tasks waiting for their turn share one card that names them: "Show the list" lists them with a "Cancel" for each, and "Cancel all" cancels the waiting ones (not the running ones). A download waiting for its retry keeps its own card.
- A catalog card being downloaded shows a ring in place of its download button: empty while waiting in the queue, filling with the download, spinning while the size is unknown or the dictionary is imported; a line under the description names the stage. ✕ inside the ring cancels it, except during the first second after tapping download, so a double tap does not cancel. The page keeps the part you look at in place while task cards appear or go away above it.
- If Screenlate is closed or stopped by the system during an import or download, that task restarts automatically and the queued ones follow; after a force stop this happens once Screenlate runs again. A download continues from where it stopped. If Screenlate closes twice while importing (not while downloading), the import is not started again and its task says so; the file may be damaged or too large for the device. Files a stopped import left behind are removed.
- If deleting a dictionary fails (for example on a full storage), a card on the Dictionaries screen says why (removing a dictionary with missing files from the start screen says it there).
- A Yomitan collection export is listed first; check the dictionaries to import (those already installed start unchecked). Large exports are read in place, without a copy. A dictionary that cannot be imported does not stop the others; the task names the ones that failed.
- Before a collection import writes anything, it checks the free space for the chosen dictionaries. The fastest import needs roughly as much free space as those dictionaries take in the export; with less, the import packs its temporary files more tightly and takes longer. If even that does not fit, the import stops with a message saying how much space is needed. With at least four times the file size free, the check is skipped.
- A dictionary's details link to its website and to where it can be downloaded, when the dictionary or the catalog names them.
- Dictionaries from the catalog whose files are missing can be downloaded again in one tap.
- The Dictionaries screen has no reset: each dictionary has its own delete, and the order, switches and sort dictionary are changed on the screen. A dictionary reset that an earlier version left unfinished completes the next time Screenlate starts, with a spinner at the top of the Dictionaries screen while it deletes the dictionaries; if Screenlate closes during that run too, the start screen says so once and the Dictionaries screen shows a card until you close it.

## Backup and restore

- **Create a backup** writes one `.zip` file wherever the file picker allows: the phone, a memory card or cloud storage. "Create backup…" first opens the list of parts: the settings sections (general, bubble and recognition, lookup, popup appearance with custom CSS, Anki, audio, translation), the order and switches of the dictionaries, the added fonts and the dictionaries themselves. Fonts and Dictionaries expand with the arrow into their files with sizes, each with its own box; the item's box shows ✓ when all its files are checked, — when some are, and is empty when none are. At first everything is checked except the dictionaries; later the list starts with the last choice, and a font or dictionary added since takes the mark of its item. "Next" opens the file picker; "Cancel" drops the changes. Dictionaries in a backup are ready to use without downloading or importing; they make the file much larger.
- With several languages turned on (or one with recognition models), a **Languages** item comes first and expands into the turned-on languages. A language left out takes out its own settings, its dictionaries, the fonts downloaded for it and its recognition models; the Fonts, Dictionaries and dictionary order items then list only what the chosen languages keep. Recognition models go in with their language and have no box of their own; a language's row shows their size. Dictionaries without a language and fonts added from files belong to every language. Languages that are not turned on are not offered, and neither are their files.
- **Restore** opens a backup and lists its parts the same way, all checked. Each checked settings section replaces what the app has; unchecked parts stay as they are. Fonts and dictionaries from the backup are added, or replace the same font or dictionary; installed ones are never removed. The dictionary order applies to the dictionaries that are installed, so restore the dictionaries themselves in the same run or install them first. A summary lists what was restored. Backups made before fonts had their own item list them under Fonts too.
- A backup with several languages lists them under Languages the same way. A language left unchecked keeps its own settings, dictionaries, fonts and recognition models. Restoring General turns the checked languages on, after the ones already on; none is turned off, and their star, flag and, when it is checked, the backup's active language come from the backup. A recognition model installed in the same version is not copied again. Backups made before languages had their own item stand for their turned-on languages (Japanese for backups of Japanese-only versions) and the languages of their dictionaries and fonts.
- A backup is for Screenlate only; Yomitan cannot read it.
- **Import from Yomitan**, the last card: a Yomitan settings export (Settings → Backup → Export Settings) brings over one profile's dictionary order and sort dictionary, Anki deck, note type, field templates and duplicate handling, audio sources, scan length, entries per lookup, text size and custom popup CSS. Dictionary switches and order apply only to the profile's language (the active language for older exports without one); other languages keep their switches and positions. A summary lists what was applied and what was skipped. A dictionary collection export is imported here too; single dictionaries (one `.zip` each) are added under Dictionaries, which the card links to.

## Anki

1. Install AnkiDroid, open Settings → Anki in Screenlate and allow access to AnkiDroid.
2. Choose a deck and a note type. Field templates are pre-filled: Senren, Lapis, Kiku, JP Mining Note, Kaishi 1.5k and the Basic Mining Deck get the templates their authors recommend (a picture field gets the screenshot, a sentence translation field the translation), other note types are filled from the field names (Word, Reading, Meaning, Picture and the like); adjust them with the + button, which inserts markers such as `{expression}`, `{reading}`, `{furigana}`, `{glossary}`, `{sentence}`, `{sentence-translation}`, `{cloze-body}`, `{audio}` and `{screenshot}`.
   - For Chinese, fields named Hanzi, Simplified, Traditional, Pinyin and English are recognized as well. `{expression-simplified}` and `{expression-traditional}` give the word in Simplified and in Traditional characters, whichever script the text was in; where a character has several forms (发: 發, 髮), the form a dictionary lists with the word's reading is used. `{furigana}` and `{sentence-furigana}` put each pinyin syllable over its character.
   - For Russian, `{furigana}` gives the word with its stress mark (ко́шка); `{sentence-furigana}` puts stress marks into the words written in their dictionary form and leaves inflected forms as written, since they may stress another syllable.
   - For a language written right to left (Arabic, Persian, Hebrew), "Fill in suggested templates" and the + button put the markers of the word, its reading (Arabic's vowelled form; in Hebrew the word itself, as the dictionaries give no reading) and the sentence in `<span dir="rtl">…</span>`, so that a card laid out left to right shows them in reading order; meanings stay as they are. The first field gets none, as the duplicate check compares its markup. Editing a field removes the span like any other text.
   - The furigana markers are offered for Japanese, Chinese and Russian; for Chinese and Russian the + menu says under each one what it gives. A field set up with them in another language is still filled.
3. The first field is used for the duplicate check. Duplicates can be searched in the collection, the deck or the deck's root, and be prevented (➕ becomes 📖, which opens the existing note; hold it to add anyway), overwritten field by field, or added again.

The AnkiDroid card at the top of the page says when AnkiDroid is missing or Screenlate has no access, with the button that grants it. **Check connection** asks AnkiDroid again and lists each part of the setup: AnkiDroid installed, access, the deck and the note type by name, and the fields against the templates (the fields the note type no longer has and its new ones). A failed item says how to fix it, and the items that need it are not checked. The check only reads; nothing changes in AnkiDroid. Once shown, the list is checked again when the deck, the note type or the fields change and when you come back to the page.

## Audio

Settings → Audio holds auto play and volume, which every language shares, and the shown language's audio sources and regions, with a test of every source on a sample word. With several languages turned on, chips at the top pick the language. The start screen's link for a language's failed audio sources opens this page on that language.

### Audio sources

Sources are listed by priority, as in Yomitan: JapanesePod101, LanguagePod101, Jisho, Lingua Libre, Wiktionary, the phone's text-to-speech (played only, never put into notes), a custom URL template with `{term}`, `{reading}` and `{language}`, and a custom URL returning Yomitan's audio source list JSON. Adding a source offers only the kinds that have the language's recordings: JapanesePod101, LanguagePod101 and Jisho for Japanese alone. All sources are asked at once and the clip of the highest source that has one plays; a source that has not answered within 5 seconds is skipped, so one that hangs does not hold up the others. A Wiktionary or Lingua Libre search that Wikimedia Commons turns down (it limits searches when too many run at once) counts as a failure, not as "no recording", so the next tap asks again. A found or played clip is kept for a minute, so playing it again needs no network. Each source has a Test button; failures say what went wrong (for example "The server refused the connection or is not running (ConnectException)", or that a custom URL returns a source list and should be a "Custom URL (JSON)" source), and sources that failed recently are listed together in one card on the start screen.

For a language spoken in several regions (English: United States, United Kingdom, other regions), the Regions list under the sources sets which Wiktionary recordings play first and in which order the popup shows transcriptions; recordings whose file names name no region count as other regions. For Latin the list holds the classical and the ecclesiastical pronunciation instead, which Wiktionary's Latin recordings and transcriptions name; recordings that name neither come after them. The list is shown while Wiktionary is among the sources or a transcription dictionary of the language is installed. Changing the region order applies to the next audio selection, including a word played recently. Lingua Libre recordings of a longer word that ends with the looked-up one (such as "kitty-cat" for "cat") come after the exact ones.

For Chinese, Wiktionary's recordings are found by the word's pinyin with tone marks, so a word read in several ways plays the reading its entry shows; Lingua Libre's are found by the characters. Wiktionary comes first by default, as more words have a recording there. Text-to-speech reads the characters.

For Arabic, Wiktionary comes first by default, then Lingua Libre, with its Modern Standard Arabic recordings only (not those of the dialects). Recordings whose file names write the word with vowel marks are found too. A recording of the word as it is written comes first from any source, then one named by the vowelled form the dictionary gives, then the rest, which may be another word of the same letters (كَلَبَ, a verb, for the noun كلب) and plays only when nothing closer is found.

For Persian, Lingua Libre comes first by default, then Wiktionary, whose Persian file names often write the word with the Arabic letters ي and ك (Fa-كتاب.ogg), so it is searched that way as well; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Hebrew, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words, then Wiktionary, whose Hebrew file names mostly write the word with vowel points (He-כֶּלֶב.ogg); those are found too, after a recording named by the word as written, since a pointed title may be another word of the same letters. The phone's voice reads the word last.

For Kazakh and Kyrgyz, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Swedish, Wiktionary comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Lingua Libre; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Norwegian, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's Bokmål voice reads the word last.

For Danish, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Romanian, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Lithuanian, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Slovak, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice reads the word last.

For Slovenian, Lingua Libre comes first by default, as it has recordings of more common words than Wiktionary; the phone's voice, where it has a Slovenian one, reads the word last.

For Esperanto, Lingua Libre comes first by default: it and Wiktionary have recordings of the common words, Lingua Libre by more speakers. The phone's voice, where it has an Esperanto one, reads the word last.

A custom URL may point to a server on the home network over plain `http://`, as in Yomitan on a computer. ## Updates and About

- Release builds look for a new version on GitHub at most once a day when the app is opened, and announce each new version once on the start screen with its changes. "Skip this version" hides it until the next release; About → Updates can still install it and has a "Check for updates" button. The announcements can be turned off there.
- "Update" downloads the APK for the device and installs it. Android asks once to allow installs from Screenlate, and asks to confirm the first update; later updates install without a prompt where Android allows it (Android 12 and newer). If the prompt did not show (the app was in the background), "Continue installing" opens it. Android closes the app when the update is installed; open it again.
- About also lists the open-source libraries, the third-party notices and the installed dictionaries with their authors and licenses, "Share logs" sends the app's log (with the app version and device model, but no looked-up text; the app keeps its own log of up to 8 MB, which also survives restarts) through the share sheet, and "Save to Downloads" writes the same file to `Download/Screenlate/`, where any file manager can open it. "Report a problem" opens a short issue form on GitHub.

## Troubleshooting

- **"This app does not allow screenshots."** The app protects its windows. Try "Read app text".
- **Only the on-device result appears.** Cloud recognition was unreachable or took longer than 15 seconds; the on-device result is used instead.
- **The bubble disappears after a while.** The system stopped the service, and the start screen says so. Apps cannot restart an accessibility service: "Bring the bubble back" opens the accessibility settings, where turning Screenlate's switch off and on again restores the bubble. To reduce how often this happens, see Settings → Background work.
- **The device gets warm or slow while scanning.** Most of a scan's work is the on-device recognition of the whole screen. Turn on "Reduce load on the device" under Text recognition; if that is not enough, choose a single engine.
- **The highlight is off or the wrong word is found with "Read app text".** The app reports incorrect character positions; turn "Read app text" off.
- **"This app does not provide its text to accessibility services."** "App text only" is on, and the app under the aim shows its text as an image or does not provide it. Turn "App text only" off to recognize the screen.
- **Kanji look Chinese.** The popup uses the phone's Japanese font; if the phone has none, the Popup settings offer to download Noto Sans JP.
- **Text-to-speech says nothing.** No voice for the language is installed; install one in the system's text-to-speech settings. For some languages (Kazakh, Kyrgyz) the phone's engine has no voice: install a text-to-speech engine that has one and choose it there.
