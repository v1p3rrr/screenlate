# Using Screenlate

## First launch

1. Screenlate first asks which language you are learning, with Japanese preselected. The chosen language opens its download screen (see Languages). Updating from a version without this question keeps Japanese and its installed dictionaries and does not ask.
2. Open Screenlate and enable its accessibility service (the start screen links to the settings). Before the settings open, a dialog says what the service reads and where it goes; "Agree" opens them, and after that the button opens them at once. About → "What the service reads and sends" shows the same text. The chosen dictionaries download and install in the background; the start screen shows when they are ready.
3. If the switch in the accessibility settings is greyed out after installing from an APK file: App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings.
4. On devices with aggressive battery management, allow Screenlate to run in the background, or the system may stop the service. Settings → Background work shows whether battery optimization is on for Screenlate, asks the system to turn it off, and opens the manufacturer's startup settings on phones that have them.

## Languages

- The active language is the one scans, search and the popup use. Each language keeps its own dictionaries and its own lookup, popup, Anki and audio settings; the other settings are shared.
- The Languages card on the start screen shows the quick languages as round flags, the active one ringed, with the active language's name and installation status; a tap on another flag makes it active. Quick languages are the active one plus up to two starred on the Languages screen. "All languages" opens that screen. With e-ink mode on, flags are replaced by two-letter language codes.
- The Languages screen lists every language Screenlate supports:
  - **Turned on**: the radio button picks the active language; the star adds a language to quick switching (the active one always has it). Switching to a starred language swaps the two: the previous active one stays starred. Switching to a language without a star (or turning one on) moves the active star to it, and the previous active language loses its star. A language spoken in several countries has a "Flag" button that picks the country of its flag (English: United States by default). "Turn off" is there while more than one language is on. Chinese also has "Characters", Simplified or Traditional: a word is shown in the glyph forms of its own script, else of the text around it, else of this choice; words found by pinyin are shown in it, and Wiktionary's Traditional entries found through a Simplified spelling show the spelling that was read. "All words in these characters" (off by default) also looks up the text converted into the chosen script and shows each word in it where a dictionary has that spelling. The default is Traditional when the interface language is Traditional Chinese, else Simplified. A backup keeps the choice with the turned-on languages; only the full settings reset returns it to the default.
  - **Turned off**: languages that left dictionaries or recognition models, with their number and size. A tap lists them with their sizes; a dictionary can be deleted alone or have its languages changed. "Delete all" deletes the language's files; "Turn on" turns it on. Languages of dictionaries this version does not support are listed here too, without "Turn on".
  - **Not turned on**: the other languages. "Turn on" turns a language on at once when its mandatory dictionaries are still installed, and opens its download screen otherwise.
- The download screen. "Languages of definitions" lists the languages the dictionaries explain words in: the interface language and English. When the interface language is English or the learned language, or has no dictionaries, a button picks another one (or none). Below, a card per category (Main dictionary, Word forms, Frequency, Pronunciation, Short translations, Characters, On-device recognition) lists the dictionaries with their size and license. On-device recognition lists the language's downloadable models (Tesseract, PaddleOCR) with the recommended one ticked and a "Main model" switch on the ticked ones; a model that needs a text detector downloads it with itself, and its size includes it. A language the built-in recognizer reads works without one; a language it does not read and without a model is recognized in the cloud only: scans need network, and with Engines set to device only a scan says the device cannot read it. Recommended ones are ticked; the only dictionary of a mandatory category is always taken; installed ones say so. ⓘ next to Pronunciation shows what such a dictionary gives. The frequency dictionary is built on the device from a word list that is downloaded instead of an archive; once imported it sorts the language's results, unless another frequency dictionary was chosen for that. The button at the bottom shows the download size, and the line under it about how much space the dictionaries take after import. It stays disabled while a mandatory category has nothing ticked or the free space is short (the space after import plus the largest archive). On mobile data, a download over 50 MB asks first.
- Confirming turns the language on and makes it active at once. Selected dictionaries already installed are enabled without downloading them again. The home screen shows the active language's readiness, on-device recognition availability, remaining installations and failures. "Installation status" opens the Dictionaries screen with the full queue, including recognition models.
- Until the mandatory dictionaries and required recognition components are ready, scanning shows an explanation and a link to installation status before reading text or capturing the screen. Typed search also shows the status while its mandatory dictionaries are missing; it searches automatically once they become ready. Optional downloads remain visible on Home after lookup is available. When on-device recognition is unavailable, cloud recognition can be used if enabled; "App text only" needs no recognition model.
- Turning a language off keeps its settings; they come back when it is turned on again. If the active language is turned off, the first starred one becomes active, otherwise the first one left. "Delete its dictionaries and models" is ticked by default and also cancels the language's unfinished downloads and bundled installation, even before the first file is installed (the size is then zero); untick it to keep the files and downloads. Kept files appear under "Turned off" on the Languages screen.

## The bubble

| Gesture | Result |
|---|---|
| Pull the bubble away from the screen edge | The screen is scanned; aim at a word to see its entry. |
| Move the floating bubble | The entry follows the aim point. The screen is not scanned again. |
| Tap the floating bubble | Scan again, e.g. after scrolling. Recognized lines flash briefly. |
| Hold the floating bubble | A menu: copy the paragraph under the aim (a whole speech bubble or paragraph, without line breaks) or all recognized text, or open Screenlate (the bubble docks). With more than one quick language, a row of their flags above (codes in e-ink mode; TalkBack reads the language names) switches the active language: the docked bubble's glyph changes at once, and the next scan (a tap on the bubble or pulling it out) uses the new language; the open scan keeps its language. With the aim off text, the paragraph of the word shown in the popup is copied. The menu opens above the bubble, or below it when there is no room above. Back, a tap anywhere outside or turning the screen off closes it; that tap does nothing else. |
| Double tap the floating bubble | Switch the aim point between "above the finger" and "bubble center". |
| Press ✕ | Close the entry; the bubble stays where it is and keeps the recognized text. The entry stays closed until the bubble moves or scans again. |
| Drag the bubble to the left or right edge | Close the entry and dock the bubble: once the bubble's center is past the edge, or when the finger is lifted at the edge. With "Top and bottom edges" on, the top and bottom edges dock it too. |
| Drag the docked bubble along the edge | Move the dock. |

A word broken at the end of a line or column is looked up as a whole, reading on in the next line or column.

When no word is found at the aim, no entry opens; if the character there is a kanji that a kanji dictionary describes, its kanji entry opens instead. The Search screen does the same for its text.

The entry opens above the word when it fits there, otherwise below the bubble, and covers neither; vertical text is treated the same. When neither has room for all of it, it keeps its size at the edge of the screen on the roomier side and covers part of the word or the bubble rather than getting low or narrow. In landscape it opens beside the word, on the side with more room, nearly as tall as the screen and a little narrower when it has to; when no side has that much room, it covers part of the word the same way.

The Quick Settings tile, the start screen and the "Show the bubble" switch in the bubble settings hide and show the bubble; holding the tile opens Screenlate. Turning the screen closes the entry and docks the bubble on the same side.

A docked bubble stays clear of the status and navigation bars and of the strips along the edges where a swipe belongs to the system (the notifications at the top, going home at the bottom), so pulling it out is never taken for a system gesture; an open keyboard lifts a bottom dock above it. Where nothing takes a touch at the screen's edge, such as the left and right edges, it sits at the very edge. In fullscreen apps, where the bars are hidden, it goes to the very edge at the top and bottom too and shows more of itself, so that a third of it stands past the system's strip: pull it out by that part. Where the strip is wider than that allows, it stays where it is with the bars shown.

## Settings

The start screen keeps the search, the service status and warnings; everything else is under Settings:

- **Bubble**: whether the bubble shows, aim point, dock side, "Top and bottom edges" (off by default: the bubble docks only at the left and right), bubble size, word highlight, vibration, apps where the bubble hides, and the text source:
  - **Screen recognition** (the default): a screenshot is recognized in the cloud; an on-device draft of the lines around the aim appears first. When cloud recognition fails (no network, no answer within 15 seconds, an error), the on-device result stays and ⚠ appears next to the text source; tap it for the reason. Cloud recognition uses mobile data, about 0.1–0.4 MB per scan.
  - **Read app text** (experimental): the app's own text is used where the app exposes character positions. It is exact, available immediately and works offline. The screen is still recognized alongside for everything else, such as text in images; whichever is ready first is shown. Some apps report incorrect character positions, which shifts the highlight or selects a neighboring word; in that case, turn the option off.
  - **App text only**: no screenshots and no recognition, only the text apps expose. Text in images, manga and games is not read, and apps that do not expose their text show a message instead. Intended for e-ink readers, older or low-end devices where recognition is unavailable or slow, and slow or metered connections. The recognition settings below are off while it is on; a picture for an Anki note is still taken when asked for.
  - **OCR boost** sends parts of the screen to cloud recognition separately, so that small text missed by a full-screen scan is recognized: on demand (the part under the aim, when nothing was found there) or always. Turn it on only if small text is not recognized: each part is an extra request and uses more mobile data.
  - **On-device model**: each turned-on language with the model it reads with; a tap opens its list: the built-in recognizer (ML Kit) where it reads the script, and the catalog models with their size, the recommended one marked, ⓘ for what each is good at. A model is downloaded from there and used once chosen; a language without a choice uses its recommended model as soon as it is installed. A model in use can be deleted only after another one is chosen; a model other languages also have lists them when it is deleted.
  - **Screen recognition → Engines**: both (the default), cloud only or device only. With both, on-device recognition reads the text around the aim first, so words are available almost immediately; the more accurate cloud result replaces it when it arrives. Without a network connection or on a cloud error, the on-device result is kept. Turning one engine off is intended for low-end or heavily loaded devices: with cloud only, nothing is recognized without a network connection and words are available only after the server responds; device only works offline but recognizes vertical text and manga less accurately, OCR boost is unavailable, and the text source shows "On-device".
  - **Screen recognition → Reduce load on the device** (off by default): the device reads the lines around the aim at once, but the whole screen only when cloud recognition has not responded within 3 seconds. Reduces load and battery use; words away from the aim become available later.
- **Lookup**: how many characters from the aim point are considered (16 by default), how many entries a lookup shows (32; larger values are slower), romaji typed or recognized as Latin text looked up as kana (taberu → たべる), and the kanji of the matched word shown as their own entries below the results. Without romaji, Latin text is looked up when Japanese follows it (Tシャツ) or when the whole word is a dictionary entry (OL, DNA, CD-ROM), wherever the aim is inside it; single letters are skipped, and letter case counts.
- **Dictionaries**, **Anki and audio**, **Popup**, **Translation**, **Appearance**: see below.
- **Background work**: battery optimization for Screenlate and the manufacturer's startup settings, the two things that can stop the bubble in the background. "Show a notification while the service runs" (under Persistent notification, off by default) keeps a minimum-priority notification; systems that stop background apps stop apps with such a notification much less often. While notifications are off for Screenlate, the service still runs but its notification is not shown; the card says so and offers to allow them.
- **Backup and restore**: backups and the import from Yomitan; see below.

The ⟲ icon at the top of the Settings list resets every setting to its default: the bubble (whether it shows, its place, size and hidden apps), lookup, Anki and audio, the popup, sentence translation, the characters chosen for Chinese, the appearance, background work and the update announcements; hints, one-time offers and the accessibility service dialog are shown again. The dictionaries, turned-on languages, installed fonts and the interface language are kept. Bubble, Lookup, Anki and audio, Popup, Translation, Appearance and Background work each have the same icon for their own settings; a page reset affects only that page, and the battery and startup settings, which belong to Android, are never changed. Every reset asks first. Holding an icon button shows what it does.

When something breaks outside Screenlate (AnkiDroid removed or its permission revoked, the note type or deck deleted, fields renamed, the accessibility service turned off, dictionary files missing, audio sources failing), the start screen says what and links to the fix; several missing dictionaries share one card, and so do a language's failed audio sources. With several languages turned on, a note setup or audio problem names its language, and its button opens that language's settings. In the popup, ➕ turns grey; tapping it shows the reason and a button that opens the Anki settings.

## Entries

- Tap a link inside a definition to look it up; the back arrow returns.
- Tap a kanji in the headword to see its kanji dictionary entry (KANJIDIC for Japanese, CC-CEDICT Hanzi for Chinese and, for Korean, a hanja dictionary built from the Unicode Han Database that comes with the app; each is ticked on its language's download screen). The entry shows the lists the character is on, such as jouyou or the basic hanja for education.
- Next to the word: how the text was inflected (🧩, the first chain of rules when several lead to the word), one frequency, the most frequent value of the first frequency dictionary (the sorting one when chosen), and up to two pitch accents or transcriptions (from pronunciation dictionaries); "+N" shows the N hidden chains, values, accents or transcriptions, "−" hides them again. A dictionary may give several values, such as one for the word written in kana (marked ㋕). A frequency dictionary's name is shown whole when it is up to four letters long (a CJK character counts as two), else as its first letter (BCCWJ → B); a tap on the chip shows the whole name, another tap shortens it again, and "+N" shows the first dictionary's name whole.
- Transcriptions carry the regions their dictionary names (US, UK, AU…) and follow the order of the Regions list of the audio sources (see Audio sources below); those without a region come last. A long list opens five at a time: "+N" shows the first five, "Show all" the rest. Transcriptions in ASCII notations (SAMPA, X-SAMPA) are left out, and a phonetic form in […] that matches a phonemic one in /…/ is shown once. Regions are read when a dictionary is imported; import an older one again to get them.
- Tap an inflection step 🧩, a pitch accent, a transcription or a tag with a description (dotted underline, or a label such as "noun" in dictionaries that describe their labels) to read the explanation at the bottom of the popup. Tag descriptions come from the dictionary's tag list and are kept for dictionaries imported from this version on; import an older dictionary again to get them.
- 🔊 plays the pronunciation from the configured audio sources; hold it to choose among all recordings found.
- ➕ adds a note to Anki; hold it to attach a picture: a crop editor opens with the word's paragraph and some space around it selected. "Whole screen" selects the whole screenshot and turns into "Frame", which brings the previous frame back.
- The note takes the sentence from the text as it is shown when ➕ is pressed. While the scan still refines its text (the spinner turns), ➕ is grey and a tap only says to wait, so no note gets its sentence from the draft; a word read from the app's own text can be added at once. A new scan closes the popup of the previous one. Docking the bubble does not stop a note already being added; an open crop editor closes as cancelled.
- Hold text in an entry to select it, drag the handles to widen the selection, and tap Copy above it; furigana are left out. Back or a tap outside the popup ends the selection.
- The copy button in an entry's header copies the word. With definition copying on (Popup settings), a copy button next to each dictionary's name copies that dictionary's definitions for the entry, without the word.

Text selected in any app can also be looked up through the "Look up in Screenlate" entry of the selection menu, and the Search screen accepts typed text.

In Chinese, the Search screen also finds words by pinyin typed in Latin letters, with or without tones: "nihao", "ni hao", "ni3hao3" and "nǐhǎo" all find 你好, and ü can be typed as v. Typed tones and an apostrophe between syllables (xi'an) rank the words that have them first; otherwise the frequency dictionary decides. Words are shown in the script chosen on the Languages screen. A dictionary imported by an earlier version must be imported again for this.

In English, a phrasal verb in the past, -ing or third-person form ("picked up", "gives up") finds its phrase even when the dictionary gives the phrase no part of speech, as Wiktionary dictionaries often do, when the word after the verb is an adverb particle (up, out, off, back and the like). After a preposition (to, for, with) the phrase is found only when the dictionary marks it as a verb, so "closed to traffic" does not give "close to" (near). A phrase with an object in between ("picked the phone up") is found only when the dictionary marks it as a verb.

In German, a compound without an entry of its own is shown as its parts in order: the longest dictionary word at its start, then the longest in the rest (Küchentischlampenschirm → Küchentisch, Lampenschirm). A linking -s-, -es- or -n- between two parts is skipped (Arbeitsschreibtisch → Arbeit, Schreibtisch). Parts are at least three letters long; a compound with its own entry is shown as it is.

In Russian, the headword shows the word with its stress marked as the dictionary gives it (ко́шка) instead of a separate reading; a note gets the word without stress marks as `{expression}` and the stressed form as `{reading}`. Text written with stress marks, or with е for ё, finds the word. Russian text is read by cloud recognition only: no on-device recognizer reads Cyrillic yet.

In Vietnamese, where each syllable is written apart, the lookup starts at the aimed syllable and at the one and two syllables before it: a word that reaches the aimed syllable is shown first (học sinh when aiming at sinh), then the syllable's own entries, and the word is highlighted from its first syllable. The old and new places of the tone mark (hoà, hòa) find the same word. Entries that only name "cái" as the classifier of a noun are left out, so "cái" shows its own entries.

Lookups also try a common respelling of the text: digits as kanji numerals (1人 → 一人), a character before 々 written twice, ッ as っ, and middle dots, commas, hyphens, periods and spaces removed. The text as it is on the screen is always looked up too.

## Popup

- **Popup elements.** "Show the recognized text above the entries" (on by default): the popup starts with the recognized text around the aim and the recognition source; off, it starts right with the entry. The translation button (see Sentence translation below), pitch accent colors or tone colors and definition copying, described below, are set in the same card; with several languages turned on, pitch accent colors are in the Japanese card and tone colors in the Chinese one.
- **Auto-hide.** Both off by default. "After adding to Anki": the popup closes once ➕ has added or updated the note, if it still shows that word. "When the aim leaves the word": while the bubble moves, the popup closes once the aim is about 3 mm away from the word, on no text (or text the current text source does not read); moving onto another word shows that word as usual. Messages (no text found, errors) stay. Only the popup closes: the bubble stays where it is and keeps the recognized text.
- **Popup font.** By default the popup and the search page use the phone's own font for the language: its Japanese font for Japanese, so kanji keep their Japanese forms (compare 直 or 骨) even where the phone's main font is Chinese, its Chinese font for Chinese, with the Simplified or Traditional forms of each word's script, and its Korean font for Korean, so hanja keep their Korean forms. Free fonts for the language can be downloaded from the list (Japanese: Noto Sans JP, Noto Serif JP, BIZ UDPGothic, BIZ UDPMincho, Klee One, Zen Maru Gothic, M PLUS 1p; Chinese: Noto Sans SC and Noto Serif SC with Simplified forms, Noto Sans TC, Noto Serif TC and LXGW WenKai TC with Traditional ones; Korean: Noto Sans KR, Noto Serif KR, Nanum Gothic, Nanum Myeongjo; languages written in Latin letters: Literata, Inter, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Lexend, PT Serif, PT Sans), or a font file (`.ttf`, `.otf`, `.ttc`) added. A language's list shows the fonts downloaded for it and every added file; a Latin font is listed, and once downloaded shown, for every language whose letters it has (Literata and Inter also cover Cyrillic, Greek and Vietnamese, Lexend Vietnamese, the PT fonts Cyrillic). A Japanese, Chinese or Korean font shows on another language's list only while it is the chosen one there. Deleting a font asks first; languages that used it go back to the phone's font. Each added file is a font of its own, named by its family and style ("Noto Serif JP Bold"); a file with the same name replaces the earlier one. Web fonts (`.woff`, `.woff2`) cannot be added: convert them to TTF or OTF first. A downloaded or added font is used only for Japanese text by default, so Latin and Cyrillic letters keep the phone's font; turn off "Only for Japanese text" (or the language's switch) to use it for everything.
- **Text size, weight and letter thickness** apply to every popup font. The weight (normal, medium, semi-bold, bold) uses the weights available in the font; a font with a single weight switches from normal directly to bold. Letter thickness draws a thin outline around each letter, which works with any font and adds to the weight. Both are off by default and follow the "Only for Japanese text" switch: with it on, or with the phone's font, they apply only to Japanese characters; with it off, to all text. The e-ink mode's "Make larger" changes only sizes.
- **Custom CSS.** Applied to the popup and the search page after the dictionaries' own styles, like Yomitan's custom popup CSS. Entries use Yomitan's class names, and badges follow Yomitan's tag color variables such as `--tag-frequency-background-color`. Warnings above the field point to lines the browser would skip and to fonts the phone does not have; such fonts fall back to the popup font. Fonts the CSS defines itself with `@font-face` are left as they are. A warning also names each server the CSS loads files from: stylesheets and fonts from the internet load (that server then sees when you look up words), other files such as images are blocked. Names of common Windows and macOS fonts stand for the phone's sans-serif or serif font of their language: Japanese (Meiryo, Yu Gothic, Yu Mincho, MS Mincho, Hiragino), Chinese (Microsoft YaHei, SimHei, SimSun, PingFang, MingLiU) and Korean (Malgun Gothic, Gulim, Dotum, Batang, Apple SD Gothic Neo).
- **Pitch accent colors** (Japanese, off by default; needs a pitch accent dictionary). The word and its reading take the color of the first pitch accent the dictionaries give, in their order, and each accent next to the word takes the color of its own type: heiban (no downstep) blue, atamadaka (downstep after the first mora) red, nakadaka (inside the word) orange, odaka (after the last mora) green, kifuku (a downstep in a verb or i-adjective) purple. Custom CSS can change them through `--pitch-heiban-color`, `--pitch-atamadaka-color`, `--pitch-nakadaka-color`, `--pitch-odaka-color` and `--pitch-kifuku-color`.
- **Tone colors** (Chinese, off by default). Chinese headwords show their pinyin one syllable above each character, and with this switch each syllable takes the color of its tone: first red, second orange, third green, fourth blue, neutral grey; the readings in a character's entry too. Custom CSS can change them through `--tone-1-color` to `--tone-5-color` (5 is the neutral tone).
- **Definition copying** (off by default) adds a copy button next to each dictionary's name in the popup and the search page. "Everything" copies all the dictionary shows for the entry: apps that paste formatted text get it formatted, others get plain text. "Only meanings" copies the numbered meanings as plain text, leaving out examples, notes, cross-references and readings; it works with dictionaries that mark their meanings (JMdict-based dictionaries, many Japanese monolingual dictionaries) and with numbered or line-per-meaning text. It is not guaranteed for every dictionary: when the meanings cannot be told apart, the whole text is copied.
- Importing a Yomitan settings export can bring over the text size and the custom popup CSS.

## Sentence translation

A free online service translates the sentence around the word, without an account or key. The translation is machine-made and may be inaccurate, and the sentence is sent to the service's server. Settings → Translation holds:

- **Translation button** (off by default; an update from version 0.2.3 or earlier, or a restored backup of such a version, keeps it on unless it was turned off). In the popup, the button sits in the header before the text source; with the recognized text hidden, it is among the first entry's buttons. A tap shows the sentence's translation under the header with the name of the service that made it and a copy button, which copies the sentence and the translation on two lines; a second tap hides it. A new word, or a popup opened again, starts without the translation; a refined scan result for the same word and sentence keeps it. The same switch is under Popup → Popup elements.
- **Translation in Anki notes** (on by default). The `{sentence-translation}` marker gets the translation the popup shows; without one, ➕ asks for it right away and the note waits for it up to 5 seconds (counted after the crop editor when ➕ is held); the field stays empty if none comes. The same switch is in the Anki settings above the deck. Pre-filled templates put the marker into a field named for a sentence translation (SentenceTranslation, Sentence Meaning, SentenceEnglish and the like; a field named only Translation stays empty); otherwise add it with the + button of a field.
- **Services**: Bing, Google and Edge, all on, in that order. The first enabled service translates the sentence; if it returns an error or does not respond within 3 seconds, the next one is used. Drag a row's handle to change the order. Bing is usually the most accurate; its first request in an hour takes about a second longer. When every service fails, the popup lists each one's reason (no connection, timeout, the service limits requests, a secure connection that failed, a certificate that is not valid for the device's date and time, and so on).
- **Translate into**: the interface language by default (English when the interface is Japanese). The list can be searched by the language's name in the interface language, in the language itself or in English, and languages can be starred to keep them at the top. Some languages are translated by only some services; the list says so.
- **Test**: asks every service that is turned on at once and shows each one's answer and how long it took.

A translation is kept for 10 minutes, so adding a note after reading the translation sends no new request.

## Appearance

- **Theme**: the system's, light or dark.
- **Colors** (Android 12 and later): Screenlate's own violet colors with a pink accent on switches, sliders, checkboxes, progress and the main buttons (the default), or System: the colors the phone picks for apps, usually from the wallpaper.
- **Language** of the interface: the system's, or English, Russian, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Polish, Turkish, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese (Simplified or Traditional). It can also be set in the system settings: Apps → Screenlate → Language. Translations were made with AI and have not been reviewed by native speakers; a note under the picker says so.
- **E-ink mode** (off by default): black and white, no animations, frames instead of translucent highlights, and a black bubble and aim; it overrides the theme. Turning it on offers a larger bubble and larger popup text; turning it off, with the switch or by restoring a backup, brings back the previous sizes, except those changed by hand in the meantime (sizes stored in the backup take precedence). On devices that look like e-ink readers the start screen offers the mode once, with a link to "App text only".

## Dictionaries

- The Dictionaries screen lists installed dictionaries by type. Drag the handle to change the order: entries from dictionaries higher in the list come first. Among frequency dictionaries, pick the one used for sorting.
- With several languages turned on, chips at the top pick the language whose dictionaries and catalog the page shows; dictionaries without a language show for every language. Dictionaries of languages that are not turned on are counted under "Other languages", with a link to the Languages screen, which lists them.
- The last enabled dictionary with definitions for a language cannot be switched off or deleted; lookups would find nothing without it. A Yomitan settings import or a backup restore that would switch them all off leaves the first of them on and says so. If none is on anyway (e.g. its files are missing), the popup and the search say so instead of "no entries found", and the search offers "Manage dictionaries".
- ⚠ on a switched on dictionary names the servers its styles load files from. Stylesheets and fonts from the internet load, and that server then sees when you look up words; other files are blocked.
- Import Yomitan archives (`.zip`) from a file, download dictionaries from the catalog, or import a Yomitan "dictionary collection" export (`.json`) to bring over everything installed in Yomitan at once; its button opens the Yomitan card of the backup screen.
- "Check for updates" asks each dictionary's index whether a newer revision exists.
- An old build of a catalog dictionary that cannot update itself (imported from elsewhere, without an update address) shows an update icon on its catalog card and is listed by "Check for updates"; updating puts the current build in its place, keeping its position and switch.
- Each dictionary shows its languages (words → explanations). They come from the dictionary's own description, else from the catalog, else from its content: the script of the headwords and definitions, with Latin text identified by the phone's on-device language detection. Dictionaries installed before this was added get theirs once, from sample lookups. Frequency and pitch dictionaries show only the language of their words. Tap a dictionary and "Change" to set them by hand; a dictionary is used only for lookups in its word language.
- Imports and downloads show their progress in a notification. On Android 13 and later the first import or download asks for the notification permission; while it is missing, the Dictionaries screen offers it again, and once Android stops asking, its button opens the app's notification settings. Before each request a short dialog says what the permission is for: the progress of imports and downloads, and keeping the bubble's service running (the switch under Background work); OK, Back or a tap outside go on to the request.
- "Cancel" on a running or waiting import or download, or in its notification, cancels only that one; the others go on. A download stops at once; a dictionary the engine is already importing is finished first and then dropped.
- Downloads wait for a network connection. A download that breaks off (network loss, timeout, server error) retries by itself up to three times, continuing from where it stopped when the server supports it; its card shows the attempt. After that, or after any other failure, the card offers "Resume" and "Cancel"; "Cancel" deletes the partial file. Imports queued after a waiting download wait for it.
- A catalog card being downloaded shows a ring in place of its download button: empty while waiting in the queue, filling with the download, spinning while the size is unknown or the dictionary is imported; a line under the description names the stage. ✕ inside the ring cancels it, except during the first second after tapping download, so a double tap does not cancel. The page keeps the part you look at in place while task cards appear or go away above it.
- If Screenlate is closed or stopped by the system during an import or download, that task restarts automatically and the queued ones follow; after a force stop this happens once Screenlate runs again. A download continues from where it stopped. If Screenlate closes twice while importing (not while downloading), the import is not started again and its task says so; the file may be damaged or too large for the device. For the bundled dictionaries this also keeps later starts of Screenlate from installing them until it is updated; "Try again" on their card installs them at once, and so does ⟲. Files a stopped import left behind are removed.
- If deleting a dictionary fails (for example on a full storage), a card on the Dictionaries screen says why (removing a dictionary with missing files from the start screen says it there).
- A Yomitan collection export is listed first; check the dictionaries to import (those already installed start unchecked). Large exports are read in place, without a copy. A dictionary that cannot be imported does not stop the others; the task names the ones that failed.
- Before a collection import writes anything, it checks the free space for the chosen dictionaries. The fastest import needs roughly as much free space as those dictionaries take in the export; with less, the import packs its temporary files more tightly and takes longer. If even that does not fit, the import stops with a message saying how much space is needed. With at least four times the file size free, the check is skipped.
- A dictionary's details link to its website and to where it can be downloaded, when the dictionary or the catalog names them.
- Dictionaries from the catalog whose files are missing can be downloaded again in one tap.
- The Dictionaries screen has no reset: each dictionary has its own delete, and the order, switches and sort dictionary are changed on the screen. A dictionary reset that an earlier version left unfinished completes the next time Screenlate starts, with a spinner at the top of the screen until it is done; if Screenlate closes during that run too, the start screen says so once and the Dictionaries screen shows a card until you close it.

## Backup and restore

- **Create a backup** writes one `.zip` file wherever the file picker allows: the phone, a memory card or cloud storage. "Create backup…" first opens the list of parts: the settings sections (general, bubble and recognition, lookup, popup appearance with custom CSS, Anki, audio, translation), the order and switches of the dictionaries, the added fonts and the dictionaries themselves. Fonts and Dictionaries expand with the arrow into their files with sizes, each with its own box; the item's box shows ✓ when all its files are checked, — when some are, and is empty when none are. At first everything is checked except the dictionaries; later the list starts with the last choice, and a font or dictionary added since takes the mark of its item. "Next" opens the file picker; "Cancel" drops the changes. Dictionaries in a backup are ready to use without downloading or importing; they make the file much larger.
- With several languages turned on (or one with recognition models), a **Languages** item comes first and expands into the turned-on languages. A language left out takes out its own settings, its dictionaries, the fonts downloaded for it and its recognition models; the Fonts, Dictionaries and dictionary order items then list only what the chosen languages keep. Recognition models go in with their language and have no box of their own; a language's row shows their size. Dictionaries without a language and fonts added from files belong to every language. Languages that are not turned on are not offered, and neither are their files.
- **Restore** opens a backup and lists its parts the same way, all checked. Each checked settings section replaces what the app has; unchecked parts stay as they are. Fonts and dictionaries from the backup are added, or replace the same font or dictionary; installed ones are never removed. The dictionary order applies to the dictionaries that are installed, so restore the dictionaries themselves in the same run or install them first. A summary lists what was restored. Backups made before fonts had their own item list them under Fonts too.
- A backup with several languages lists them under Languages the same way. A language left unchecked keeps its own settings, dictionaries, fonts and recognition models. Restoring General turns the checked languages on, after the ones already on; none is turned off, and their star, flag and, when it is checked, the backup's active language come from the backup. A recognition model installed in the same version is not copied again. Backups made before languages had their own item stand for their turned-on languages (Japanese for backups of Japanese-only versions) and the languages of their dictionaries and fonts.
- A backup is for Screenlate only; Yomitan cannot read it.
- **Import from Yomitan**, the last card: a Yomitan settings export (Settings → Backup → Export Settings) brings over one profile's dictionary order and sort dictionary, Anki deck, note type, field templates and duplicate handling, audio sources, scan length, entries per lookup, text size and custom popup CSS. Dictionary switches and order apply only to the profile's language (the active language for older exports without one); other languages keep their switches and positions. A summary lists what was applied and what was skipped. A dictionary collection export is imported here too; single dictionaries (one `.zip` each) are added under Dictionaries, which the card links to.

## Anki

1. Install AnkiDroid, open Anki and audio settings in Screenlate and allow access to AnkiDroid.
2. Choose a deck and a note type. Field templates are pre-filled: Senren, Lapis, Kiku, JP Mining Note, Kaishi 1.5k and the Basic Mining Deck get the templates their authors recommend (a picture field gets the screenshot, a sentence translation field the translation), other note types are filled from the field names (Word, Reading, Meaning, Picture and the like); adjust them with the + button, which inserts markers such as `{expression}`, `{reading}`, `{furigana}`, `{glossary}`, `{sentence}`, `{sentence-translation}`, `{cloze-body}`, `{audio}` and `{screenshot}`.
   - For Chinese, fields named Hanzi, Simplified, Traditional, Pinyin and English are recognized as well. `{expression-simplified}` and `{expression-traditional}` give the word in Simplified and in Traditional characters, whichever script the text was in; where a character has several forms (发: 發, 髮), the form a dictionary lists with the word's reading is used. `{furigana}` and `{sentence-furigana}` put each pinyin syllable over its character.
3. The first field is used for the duplicate check. Duplicates can be searched in the collection, the deck or the deck's root, and be prevented (➕ becomes 📖, which opens the existing note; hold it to add anyway), overwritten field by field, or added again.

The AnkiDroid card at the top of the page says when AnkiDroid is missing or Screenlate has no access, with the button that grants it. **Check connection** asks AnkiDroid again and lists each part of the setup: AnkiDroid installed, access, the deck and the note type by name, and the fields against the templates (the fields the note type no longer has and its new ones). A failed item says how to fix it, and the items that need it are not checked. The check only reads; nothing changes in AnkiDroid. Once shown, the list is checked again when the deck, the note type or the fields change and when you come back to the page.

### Audio sources

Sources are listed by priority, as in Yomitan: JapanesePod101, LanguagePod101, Jisho, Lingua Libre, Wiktionary, the phone's text-to-speech (played only, never put into notes), a custom URL template with `{term}`, `{reading}` and `{language}`, and a custom URL returning Yomitan's audio source list JSON. Adding a source offers only the kinds that have the language's recordings: JapanesePod101, LanguagePod101 and Jisho for Japanese alone. All sources are asked at once and the clip of the highest source that has one plays; a source that has not answered within 5 seconds is skipped, so one that hangs does not hold up the others. A found or played clip is kept for a minute, so playing it again needs no network. Each source has a Test button; failures say what went wrong (for example "The server refused the connection or is not running (ConnectException)", or that a custom URL returns a source list and should be a "Custom URL (JSON)" source), and sources that failed recently are listed together in one card on the start screen.

For a language spoken in several regions (English: United States, United Kingdom, other regions), the Regions list under the sources sets which Wiktionary recordings play first; recordings whose file names name no region count as other regions. Changing the region order applies to the next audio selection, including a word played recently. Lingua Libre recordings of a longer word that ends with the looked-up one (such as "kitty-cat" for "cat") come after the exact ones.

For Chinese, Wiktionary's recordings are found by the word's pinyin with tone marks, so a word read in several ways plays the reading its entry shows; Lingua Libre's are found by the characters. Wiktionary comes first by default, as more words have a recording there. Text-to-speech reads the characters.

A custom URL may point to a server on the home network over plain `http://`, as in Yomitan on a computer. When such a server's list names its files by a local address (`0.0.0.0`, `localhost`, `127.0.0.1`), the files are fetched from the list's server instead. On Android 17 and newer, a source on the home network needs the nearby devices (local network) permission: the app asks for it when such a source is added, imported from Yomitan or restored from a backup, and a failed test offers it again.

## Updates and About

- Release builds look for a new version on GitHub at most once a day when the app is opened, and announce each new version once on the start screen with its changes. "Skip this version" hides it until the next release; About → Updates can still install it and has a "Check for updates" button. The announcements can be turned off there.
- "Update" downloads the APK for the device and installs it. Android asks once to allow installs from Screenlate, and asks to confirm the first update; later updates install without a prompt where Android allows it (Android 12 and newer). If the prompt did not show (the app was in the background), "Continue installing" opens it. Android closes the app when the update is installed; open it again.
- About also lists the open-source libraries, the third-party notices and the installed dictionaries with their authors and licenses, "Share logs" sends the app's log (with the app version and device model, but no looked-up text) through the share sheet, and "Save to Downloads" writes the same file to `Download/Screenlate/`, where any file manager can open it. "Report a problem" opens a short issue form on GitHub.

## Troubleshooting

- **"This app does not allow screenshots."** The app protects its windows. Try "Read app text".
- **Only the on-device result appears.** Cloud recognition was unreachable or took longer than 15 seconds; the on-device result is used instead.
- **The bubble disappears after a while.** The system stopped the service, and the start screen says so. Apps cannot restart an accessibility service: "Bring the bubble back" opens the accessibility settings, where turning Screenlate's switch off and on again restores the bubble. To reduce how often this happens, see Settings → Background work.
- **The device gets warm or slow while scanning.** Most of a scan's work is the on-device recognition of the whole screen. Turn on "Reduce load on the device" in the bubble settings; if that is not enough, choose a single engine.
- **The highlight is off or the wrong word is found with "Read app text".** The app reports incorrect character positions; turn "Read app text" off.
- **"This app does not provide its text to accessibility services."** "App text only" is on, and the app under the aim shows its text as an image or does not provide it. Turn "App text only" off to recognize the screen.
- **Kanji look Chinese.** The popup uses the phone's Japanese font; if the phone has none, the Popup settings offer to download Noto Sans JP.
- **Text-to-speech says nothing.** No voice for the language is installed; install one in the system's text-to-speech settings.
